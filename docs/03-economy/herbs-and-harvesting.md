# Medicinal Herbs and Harvesting

Status: PROVISIONAL foundation.

## Core principle

Medicinal plants are not a linear rarity ladder.

Common herbs remain relevant at high progression because each plant has a distinct medical role. Rare herbs expand treatment options rather than replacing common ones.

Herbs do **not** use the five craftsmanship quality tiers.

Plant expertise should matter through:
- identification;
- harvest yield;
- waste reduction;
- preservation;
- contamination detection;
- correct plant-part selection;
- regrowth preservation;
- rare-resource discovery.

## Medicinal property model

Herbs may contribute one or more functional properties such as:
- Hemostatic;
- Wound Care;
- Anti-inflammatory;
- Restorative;
- Absorbent;
- Pain Control;
- Calming;
- Pall-reactive / specialist medicinal effect.

These are design properties used by recipes and treatments, not necessarily all exposed as raw numeric stats to players.

## Phase One medicinal plants

### Yarrow

Rarity: Common.

Primary role:
- Hemostatic: High
- Wound Care: Medium
- Anti-inflammatory: Low

Endgame relevance:
Yarrow remains a core bleeding-control ingredient even in advanced trauma dressings.

Possible sources:
- city outskirts;
- managed medicinal gardens;
- safe fields;
- Grey Marches.

### Calendula

Rarity: Common-Uncommon.

Primary role:
- Wound Care: High
- Anti-inflammatory: Medium
- Hemostatic: Low

Endgame relevance:
Used in higher-grade wound-care preparations where stopping bleeding alone is insufficient.

### Nettle

Rarity: Common.

Primary role:
- Restorative: Medium
- Nutritional: High
- Anti-inflammatory: Low

Endgame relevance:
Useful as a cheap restorative base in advanced medicines, expedition foods and recovery preparations.

### Comfrey

Rarity: Common-Uncommon.

Primary role:
- Trauma Recovery: High
- Wound Care: Medium
- Internal use: Unsafe / restricted

Endgame relevance:
Specialized limb, sprain and blunt-trauma treatments.

### Sphagnum Moss

Rarity: Uncommon-Rare locally.

Primary role:
- Absorbent: Exceptional
- Wound Care: Medium

Endgame relevance:
Advanced trauma dressings and expedition medical kits.

Possible sources:
- clean bogs;
- wet Grey Marches pockets;
- rare protected marsh sites.

### Black Turmeric

Resource identity:
Black Turmeric / Curcuma caesia. "Blue turmeric" is treated as the same plant rather than a separate resource.

Rarity: Very Rare.

Primary role:
- Anti-inflammatory: Exceptional
- Restorative: High
- Medicine potency/catalyst: High

Endgame relevance:
High-end recovery compounds and specialist medicines.

Possible sources:
- rare warm microclimates;
- abandoned botanical estates;
- surviving glasshouses;
- unusual Grey Marches pockets.

### Agarwood

Agarwood is resinous heartwood, not technically an herb.

Rarity: Rare.

Primary role:
- Calming: Exceptional
- Medicine catalyst: Medium
- Luxury value: Exceptional

Acquisition:
Agarwood can appear as a rare find while logging or inspecting damaged/resinous trees.

It should not have a dedicated "Gather Agarwood" node.

Higher Fieldcraft, Carpentry or Herbalism can reveal telltale resinous wood that an inexperienced logger may overlook.

Uses:
- Composure medicine;
- high-end physician preparations;
- luxury/incense/perfume trade;
- specialist research.

### Devil's Claw

Rarity: Rare / Pall-zone only.

Primary role:
- Anti-inflammatory: Very High
- Pain Control: Exceptional
- Pall-adapted specialist ingredient

Lore direction:
A pre-Pall botanical species survived or adapted in contaminated dry regions and is now found only in dangerous Pall environments.

Endgame relevance:
Pain-control and trauma medicines that let an injured character function better without pretending the underlying injury is healed.

## Shared-world harvesting

Herb patches should generally be **shared world resources**, not personally instanced per player.

Reasons:
- supports discovery;
- creates meaningful scarcity;
- gives location knowledge value;
- enables player competition without forced PvP;
- makes rare-resource rumours matter;
- supports a real market.

However, a rare herb should not be represented as a single binary plant that disappears forever when one player clicks it.

Instead, the world tracks a **harvest patch/resource population**.

A patch can have:
- plant species;
- current harvestable amount;
- maturity;
- regeneration rate;
- depletion state;
- contamination state;
- local environmental modifiers;
- recent harvesting pressure.

## Harvest pressure and regrowth

A patch can be harvested in different ways.

### Careful harvest
- lower immediate yield;
- low damage to the patch;
- high chance the plant/population remains productive;
- may require Herbalism knowledge.

### Standard harvest
- normal yield;
- moderate regrowth impact.

### Aggressive harvest
- higher immediate yield;
- severe depletion;
- long recovery;
- possible temporary collapse of the patch.

For root/rhizome plants such as Black Turmeric, taking the entire root may destroy that individual plant.

A knowledgeable harvester may have an option to:
- take only part of the rhizome;
- leave viable growth material;
- mark the location for later return.

Another player can still arrive later and harvest aggressively.

This is intentional: the resource exists in a shared world.

## Anti-grief / anti-extinction guardrails

Shared harvesting should create competition, not permanent server damage.

Therefore:
- common resources regenerate reliably;
- rare patches can become Exhausted rather than permanently deleted;
- severe overharvesting causes longer recovery, not endless extinction;
- world events/procedural ecology can create new patches elsewhere;
- some rare-resource locations may migrate or reappear after long depletion;
- tutorial/critical progression herbs must always have alternative sources.

No guild should be able to permanently erase Yarrow or Black Turmeric from the server.

## Novice vs expert harvesting

Expertise should create a very noticeable difference.

A novice may:
- misidentify plants;
- collect the wrong plant part;
- damage usable material;
- leave usable material behind;
- fail to notice contamination;
- damage the patch;
- miss hidden rare plants.

An expert may:
- identify species immediately;
- estimate maturity and contamination;
- select the most useful plant part;
- recover much more usable material;
- preserve regrowth;
- detect rare companion plants;
- choose efficient tools/methods.

Illustrative yield direction for the same healthy patch:
- novice: 40-60% of potential usable yield;
- competent: 65-80%;
- expert: 85-100%+ through byproduct discovery or exceptional technique.

Exact values remain balance data.

Expertise should not literally create matter. Higher yield represents less waste, better selection and recovery.

## Harvest interaction example

A player finds a Black Turmeric patch.

Novice view:
- "Dark-leaved plant with a thick rhizome."
- Pull plant.
- Cut root.
- Leave it.

Expert Herbalism view:
- Black Turmeric.
- Mature rhizome.
- No visible Pall contamination.
- Full uprooting will kill this plant.
- Partial rhizome harvest is viable.

Expert options:
- take a small section and preserve regrowth;
- take a normal medicinal portion;
- uproot the entire plant for maximum immediate yield;
- mark the patch and leave it untouched.

This makes skill change understanding and stewardship, not merely success chance.

## Herb quality

Do not use Crude / Standard / Well-made / Fine / Masterwork for harvested herbs.

Those tiers describe craftsmanship quality, not natural plant quality.

Herb variation should normally remain at the source/condition level, for example:
- healthy vs immature;
- clean vs contaminated;
- fresh vs badly damaged.

Only variation that changes gameplay should be tracked.

Avoid a proliferation of:
- Fine Yarrow;
- Masterwork Nettle;
- Rare Calendula;
- etc.

If a recipe needs a stronger medicinal product, quality should usually emerge from:
- correct plant;
- good harvesting;
- processing;
- crafter/doctor skill;
- facility;
- recipe;
- additional specialist ingredients.

## Item-state simplicity

Phase One should keep harvested herbs mostly fungible.

Suggested rule:
- one normal item per herb species;
- optional Contaminated variant only where contamination creates a real processing/safety decision;
- no universal potency/quality tiers.

Maturity, health and plant condition primarily affect harvest yield before the item enters inventory.

## Example advanced recipe philosophy

Advanced Expedition Trauma Kit may use:
- Yarrow for bleeding control;
- Sphagnum Moss for absorbency;
- Calendula for wound treatment;
- Comfrey for trauma recovery;
- Devil's Claw extract for pain/inflammation;
- Fine Cloth;
- Antiseptic.

Rare ingredients expand the treatment profile, while common Yarrow remains essential.

## Design guardrail

The harvesting system should create the feeling:

"I know how to take more from this landscape without ruining it."

not:

"My Herbalism number gives me +27% herbs."
