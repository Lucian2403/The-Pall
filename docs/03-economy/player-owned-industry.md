# Player-Owned Industry and Employment

Status: PROVISIONAL foundation.

## Core idea

Late-game players, companies or guilds may own or lease productive infrastructure such as:
- quarry;
- coal pit;
- timber yard;
- refinery;
- foundry;
- large workshop;
- warehouse;
- later, rail/logistics facilities.

Ownership should create an economic role, not passive infinite income.

A productive site requires:
- capital investment;
- upkeep;
- tools/machinery;
- fuel where relevant;
- storage;
- labor;
- logistics;
- access rights/lease/tax where appropriate;
- a market for its output.

## Player-posted jobs

Owners can post work shifts/contracts for other players.

Example:

**Blackridge Quarry — Stone Cutting Shift**
- Duration: 20 minutes
- Capacity: 6 workers
- Base wage: 180 coins
- Performance bonus: up to 60 coins
- Worker keeps: small scrap/byproduct allowance, if enabled
- Skill XP: Industry / Hauling / Construction as relevant
- Requirements: STR 7 or Industry 5
- Tools: supplied by quarry / worker supplies own, depending on job
- Risk: low

Wages should be escrowed when the owner posts the job so workers cannot be scammed after completing it.

## Worker experience

Working at a player-owned site must still be gameplay, not “click work -> wait.”

The same job interaction principles apply:
- routine work may run mostly uninterrupted for experienced workers;
- difficult, novel or poorly maintained sites may generate early interactions/incidents;
- workers can make choices affecting output, safety, wear or efficiency;
- skill changes available information and options.

Workers receive:
- wages;
- skill XP;
- reputation or employer history where appropriate;
- occasional byproducts/bonuses;
- possibly advancement into better jobs at that facility.

## Owner economics

The owner earns through the margin between:
- value of produced goods

and:
- wages;
- maintenance;
- taxes/lease;
- fuel;
- replacement parts;
- transport;
- facility wear;
- losses/incidents.

The site should not create resources from nothing without labor or machinery cycles.

High-level automation may later reduce labor needs, but should require expensive machinery, fuel, maintenance and specialist oversight.

## Job market competition

Owners choose:
- wage;
- shift size;
- requirements;
- optional bonuses;
- supplied tools;
- work schedule.

Workers choose employers.

A badly paying quarry may sit idle while a competitor fills every shift.

This creates a real labor market.

Commerce/Industry reputation may help owners estimate competitive wages, output value and staffing needs.

## Anti-snowball guardrails

Large wealth should create capability, not guaranteed exponential wealth.

Possible constraints:
- high acquisition/lease cost;
- finite site throughput;
- significant upkeep;
- limited worker capacity;
- location/logistics costs;
- resource-site quality differences;
- market saturation;
- taxes/permits;
- machinery depreciation;
- safety incidents when under-maintained;
- owner must stock wage escrow in advance.

Owning five empty quarries should be worse than owning one well-run quarry.

## Ownership models

Possible models:
- personal ownership;
- long-term city lease;
- guild/company ownership;
- temporary extraction concession.

The first implementation can begin with personal or guild ownership and expand later.

## Resource rights

Player-owned resource sites should not necessarily provide exclusive control over an entire global resource.

For example, Stone remains available from:
- NPC/city baseline supply;
- public jobs;
- other quarries;
- exploration locations;
- civic production.

A player quarry is an efficient production opportunity, not a monopoly over Stone itself.

## Example progression

Early player:
- works shifts at municipal quarry.

Mid-game player:
- buys tools;
- takes higher-paying specialist quarry work;
- possibly leases a small extraction site.

Late-game player:
- buys/leases an independent quarry;
- installs machinery;
- posts paid shifts;
- sells stone/building materials;
- hires haulers;
- upgrades throughput;
- eventually supplies large construction projects or rail works.

This turns wealth into infrastructure and player employment rather than purely better personal gear.
