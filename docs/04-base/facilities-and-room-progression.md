# Facilities and Room Progression

Status: PROVISIONAL foundation.

## Core principle

A better room or workshop should not simply give +10% crafting.

Facility progression should expand what the player can actually do through:
- batch capacity;
- recipe complexity;
- quality ceiling;
- safety;
- efficiency;
- processing speed;
- storage;
- contamination control;
- machine/tool capacity;
- worker or automation support later.

The player's skill and the facility are separate requirements.

A highly skilled doctor working from a crude hut bench remains constrained by the facility.

## Room vs workstation

A **room/module** provides space and infrastructure.

A **workstation** is installed inside an appropriate room and enables specific activities.

Examples:
- spare hut room -> crude medical bench;
- workshop room -> medicine workshop;
- dedicated clinic/laboratory -> advanced medical production.

This lets housing growth matter without making every new recipe require a completely new building.

## Facility limits

Facilities can constrain production in several independent ways.

### 1. Batch size

Example medical progression:
- improvised medicine bench: 10 bandages per batch;
- proper medicine workshop: 25;
- clinic preparation room: 50;
- advanced medical laboratory: 100.

Batch capacity should matter mostly for bulk goods.

A better facility does not necessarily let the player produce 100 advanced respirators at once.

### 2. Recipe complexity

Some facilities can only perform simple processes.

Example:
- crude bench: bandages, basic herbal preparations;
- workshop: antiseptic, field medicine, simple medical components;
- clinic/lab: sterile preparations, advanced Pall treatments, complex compounds.

### 3. Quality ceiling

Facility quality can cap the highest item quality achievable even if the player has enough skill.

Example:
- crude bench: Crude / Standard;
- proper workshop: up to Well-made;
- advanced specialist facility: Fine;
- top-end specialist facility: Masterwork-eligible where the recipe itself supports it.

The facility enables the attempt; skill, materials and process still determine the result.

### 4. Throughput / production time

Higher facilities may:
- reduce setup time;
- process larger batches efficiently;
- reduce heating/cooling/sterilization time;
- run better machinery.

Time savings should be meaningful but not extreme.

### 5. Material efficiency

Better workspaces reduce avoidable waste through:
- cleaner work areas;
- precise measurement;
- better fixtures;
- reliable temperature/pressure;
- proper storage.

Skill and facility efficiency stack but neither should make essential material costs disappear.

### 6. Safety

Facilities can reduce:
- injury chance;
- contamination incidents;
- fires;
- chemical accidents;
- ruined batches;
- equipment damage.

Risky recipes may be prohibited entirely below a required safety level.

### 7. Contamination control

Especially important for medicine, Pall research and food.

Possible capabilities:
- clean work surface;
- sealed storage;
- ventilation;
- decontamination sink;
- pressure isolation;
- sterile preparation area.

A contaminated workshop may be usable for metalwork but unsuitable for surgery or high-grade medicine.

### 8. Tool / machine capacity

A crude room may support one small workstation.

Larger facilities may support:
- multiple benches;
- furnaces;
- presses;
- pressure systems;
- precision machinery;
- sterilizers;
- storage racks.

This limits what can physically be installed.

### 9. Input and output storage

Higher-tier facilities can hold more:
- raw inputs;
- components;
- fuel;
- work-in-progress;
- finished batches.

Overflow goes to general storage or blocks production.

### 10. Fuel / utility requirements

Advanced facilities may require:
- coal;
- oil;
- water;
- steam pressure;
- ventilation;
- electricity if the setting eventually supports limited generation;
- replacement machine parts.

This creates operating cost.

### 11. Maintenance burden

Better machinery is more capable but requires:
- lubrication;
- replacement parts;
- calibration;
- cleaning;
- specialist servicing.

Advanced facilities should not be free passive upgrades.

### 12. Independent machine processes

At higher progression, some facilities may run processes independently of the player's personal activity:
- drying;
- fermentation;
- refining;
- sterilization;
- furnace cycles;
- machine-driven bulk production.

This does not mean unrestricted queues.

The character still performs setup and any required skilled interaction, but the machine can continue after that.

Worker support may expand this later.

## Example: Medicine facility progression

### Tier 1 — Improvised Medicine Bench

Fits in the rented hut.

Capabilities:
- batch size around 10 for simple consumables;
- bandages;
- simple herbal preparations;
- basic antiseptic work;
- Crude/Standard output;
- high material waste;
- no sterile or Pall-sensitive recipes;
- limited storage.

Cheap and accessible.

### Tier 2 — Medicine Workshop

Requires a larger dedicated room or upgraded dwelling.

Capabilities:
- batch size around 25;
- proper field medicines;
- sterile dressings;
- simple medical components;
- improved storage;
- reduced waste;
- safer processing;
- Well-made-capable where recipes permit.

### Tier 3 — Clinic Preparation Room

Requires Workshop House / Compound-level space.

Capabilities:
- batch size around 50;
- advanced medicines;
- controlled sterilization;
- basic Pall-related medicine;
- Fine-capable for appropriate recipes;
- strong contamination control;
- dedicated medical storage.

### Tier 4 — Medical Laboratory

High-end specialist facility.

Capabilities:
- batch size around 100 for appropriate bulk goods;
- complex Pall treatments;
- high-risk compounds;
- Fine / Masterwork-eligible recipes where supported;
- sealed storage;
- advanced sterilization;
- very low process waste;
- machine-assisted processes;
- high maintenance and operating cost.

The facility does not create medical skill. It allows a skilled player to use their knowledge at scale.

## Specialization pressure

Property space should be limited.

A player may own enough room for:
- forge + warehouse + kitchen

or:
- medical lab + chemistry room + sealed storage

but not every advanced facility simultaneously without reaching very large estate progression.

This turns housing into an economic specialization system rather than a cosmetic ladder.

## Design guardrail

Facility tiers should not make earlier facilities meaningless.

A crude bench remains useful for cheap small jobs, emergency repairs and low-volume production.

Large facilities are better when scale, complexity, quality or safety matter.


## Phase One Textile & Leather Workshop

A single **Textile & Leather Workshop** supports Craftsmanship -> Tailoring processes for both Cloth and Leather.

It represents a compact work area containing the appropriate hand tools and small equipment for:
- fibre preparation/spinning/weaving;
- cutting and sewing;
- hide scraping/stretching;
- small-scale tanning/drying.

It is one gameplay workstation/facility family, not one literal machine.

This keeps Phase One property pressure reasonable while preserving believable physical processes.


## Phase One Oil Distillation Rig

Status: PROVISIONAL.

The **Oil Distillation Rig** is a specialist Industry -> Refining workstation for Crude Oil processing.

It is not part of the Forge/Smelter line.

Recommended placement:
- requires a proper dedicated industrial room, approximately Workshop House progression or better;
- unavailable in the starter hut;
- supports sealed liquid input/output storage;
- requires ventilation and fire-safety capability.

The workstation represents a compact retort/still, condenser, valves, collection vessels and filtration/settling hardware.

### Facility progression

Oil refining uses a shorter specialist facility ladder than metalworking:

| Tier | Facility | Recommended Refining | Max output batch |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| T1 | Basic Distillation Rig | 30 | 5 |
| T2 | Sealed Distillation Rig | 50 | 20 |
| T3 | Industrial Distillation Plant | 70 | 50 |
| T4 | Large Refinery | LATER | TBD |

Tier improvements primarily provide:
- larger batches;
- safer pressure and vapor handling;
- better condensation/recovery;
- lower avoidable fuel loss;
- lower incident chance;
- stronger contamination control;
- better liquid storage integration.

Facility upgrades do not remove the recipe's unavoidable 4:1 Crude Oil conversion loss.

A T4 refinery is deliberately deferred until player-owned industrial scale warrants it.

Its purpose is to let Refining progression branch beyond metalworking without making the Forge a universal processing station.


### Canister component workstation mapping

Phase One component recipes use existing workstation families:

- Metal Plate -> Forge, T1 sufficient;
- Copper Fitting -> Forge, T1 sufficient;
- Seal Gasket -> Textile & Leather Workshop, T1 sufficient.

The progression gate for Copper Fitting and Seal Gasket comes from their raw skill requirements, not from forcing an unnecessary higher workstation tier.

Final Filter Canister assembly uses the Mechanics -> Pressure Systems workstation.

Canonical workstation name: **Pressure Bench**.

Canister access by facility tier:
- T1: Crude and Grey Filter Canisters;
- T2: no additional canister tier required, but the tier may unlock other Pressure Systems recipes;
- T3: Pall Filter Canister;
- T4: Deep Pall Filter Canister.

Facility tiers are not defined solely by Filter Canisters; other named Pressure Systems items can justify T2 and later progression.
