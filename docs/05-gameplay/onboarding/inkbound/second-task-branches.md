## Route A — The Coal Waiver (Commerce / Contracts)

**Player chose [Challenge the order].** The Inkbound goes to an ordinary **fuel-allocation counter**, where the coal order and appeals are recorded. The job is **checking which paper says what**, not working out legal theory or acting as a court.

### Work A1 — Find the matching papers

**[Ask to see the Ward Six papers]** — an unnamed counter clerk brings out the heating reduction order and a letter from residents asking that their coal not be cut.

**[Compare the two papers]** — the player can see:
- The **coal reduction** bears the mark of the office that issued it.
- The residents' letter is marked **RECEIVED**.
- A stamped **RECEIVED** mark proves only that someone took the letter, **not that anyone approved or even considered the request**.
- The winter-loss calculation beside the order does not, by itself, carry the authority to dismiss a separate appeal. The player does not need to know bureaucratic jargon to spot the difference.

*The counter clerk points to the appeal entry.*

**CLERK:** “We received their letter. See? It's in the book.”

**PLAYER:** “Then what did they decide?”

**CLERK:** “Nothing yet.”

**PLAYER:** “These people are asking for coal now.”

**CLERK:** “I'm aware of that.”

### Work A2 — Check why nothing has happened

**[Look at the date for review]** — the office book states that **winter appeals are to be left without a decision until spring**. The player can read the instruction directly. It is **deliberate delay**, not merely an overworked clerk failing to find one page. Do not invent which senior official ordered this or treat the calculation as lawful authority.

**PLAYER:** “Spring? That'll be after the winter.”

**CLERK:** “Yes.”

**PLAYER:** “And you're content to leave it there?”

**CLERK:** “It's where I'm told to leave it. You can write an objection if you like.”

### Work A3 — Make a truthful note

Choose one ordinary Contracts approach:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **[Ask for the written reason]** | The clerk shows the instruction postponing appeals; the Inkbound records that the application is waiting, not approved |
| **[Put the delay in writing]** | Enter an objection noting that the heating appeal was received but is being held until spring |

Both lead to **[Enter the disputed status]**: the counter records a note that the residents' winter heating request is still unanswered and the delay is challenged. It does **not** approve coal, clear a payment, cancel the order or free the locked communal supply. The official note/copy stays with the fuel office and in the journal; the player's separate short working notes from the shared Stage 4 handoff are only untradeable scene props.

**CLERK:** “I'll note the objection. I can't change the order.”

**PLAYER:** “Then write that too.”

**Branch finding:** **The appeal was received and intentionally left unanswered until spring.** The player has an appeal reference and an honest dispute entry; a ward full of households is still cold.

## Route B — The Door Count (Fieldcraft / Navigation)

**Player chose [Check the houses].** The Inkbound joins an unnamed route worker in Ward Six to **count real occupied doors and check whether a coal cart can reach them**. This is ordinary Navigation/observation work: look where you walk, note blocked paths, find another way around. Not a break-in, spy mission or high-skill map puzzle.

### Work B1 — Check the listed addresses

**[Take the address list]** — compare a short list of houses recorded as empty or nearly empty with the buildings standing on the actual street.

**[Walk to the marked doors]** — several have inhabitants, visible lamps, people answering or washing hung in the passages. **Do not equate a closed door with an empty house**; allow **[Knock and check]**, with safe observation rather than forced entry.

*At a downstairs doorway, a resident answers.*

**RESIDENT:** “What do you want?”

**PLAYER:** “I'm checking which houses are lived in. The city's got this one down as empty.”

**RESIDENT:** “Empty? We've been here all winter.”

*Inside, the player sees a tenant burning scraps from a pulled-up floorboard in the stove.*

**PLAYER:** “You're burning the floor?”

**RESIDENT:** “Coal's gone. What else am I meant to put in there?”

The player may not know the resident's name or exact household size. The evidence is local and physical: **these houses are occupied despite what the books say, and at least one household is burning its own floorboards for heat**.

### Work B2 — Check a way in for a cart

At a yard entrance, piles of rubbish block the shortest way into the row of homes.

**[Look at both routes]** — one goes straight through the obstructed courtyard; the other is longer but open.

| Action | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **[Check the short courtyard way]** | Discover it is too blocked for a coal cart; mark the obstruction before going round |
| **[Take the open street]** | Walk farther but find a passable cart route without needing to move anybody's property |

**[Mark the usable turns]** and **[Walk the route back]** — check that a loaded cart could reach the houses. Chalk and notes belong to the route service, not the player's starter inventory. Do not teleport a delivery or claim every lane is safe for all future loads.

*The route worker looks at the street the Inkbound has marked.*

**ROUTE WORKER:** “A cart'll get down here.”

**PLAYER:** “Not the yard way?”

**ROUTE WORKER:** “Not unless somebody clears it. This way's open.”

**Branch finding:** **The supposed empty buildings are occupied; a resident is burning floorboards; a workable coal-cart route is marked.** This is evidence and access, **not** an automatic coal delivery or the relocation of all the residents.

**[Hand over the route notes]** returns the original mapping sheet to the route worker. The player keeps only their own short handwritten summary of the passable street and occupied doors, following the shared Stage 4 work-note rule. Normal Navigation jobs of this kind are ordinarily paid; this visit was not a commissioned survey.

## Route C — The Short Cart (Industry / Hauling)

**Player chose [Follow the coal delivery].** The Inkbound goes to the ordinary fuel-cart loading area to help an unnamed driver with a **small scheduled consignment bound for Ward Six**. This teaches **checking a load, keeping a cart safe and confirming where goods are left**—not doing paperwork investigations in another outfit.

### Work C1 — Check what was loaded

**[Read the delivery slip]** — it names Ward Six and the amount of coal **supposed** to be on the cart.

**[Count the coal sacks]** — fewer sacks are present than the slip requires. The discrepancy is **large enough to leave households short**, though exact weights/counts depend on the eventual job data. The player doesn't discover who removed coal or why the cart was underfilled.

*The driver looks into the cart.*

**DRIVER:** “That's all they've put on.”

**PLAYER:** “The loaders were supposed to put more on.”

**DRIVER:** “Then go and ask them why they didn't. This is all I've got.”

**PLAYER:** “You knew it was short?”

**DRIVER:** “I know what they loaded. I don't know where the rest went.”

This is the driver's plain irritation, not a confession of theft or proof of a new smuggling ring.

### Work C2 — Make the smaller load safe to carry

**[Check how the sacks are stacked]** — the underfilled cart needs its remaining load kept steady, and the player can see a pile leaning to one side.

Choose an ordinary Hauling action:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **[Shift the sacks so the weight sits evenly]** | More handling, but safer travel |
| **[Keep the load as it is]** | Starts sooner but may shift on rough ground; **[Stop and straighten the load]** handles the visible problem if it occurs |

No hauling skill gate strands the new character. The driver and the cart remain responsible for transporting their own coal. No free personal sack, cart, lifting equipment or tool is granted.

### Work C3 — Deliver what is there and record the shortage

**[Choose a passable delivery street]** — compare an obvious shorter but cramped approach with a longer street a loaded cart can use. If the player attempts the cramped way, the driver stops and the job allows **[Take the wider street]**, costing time rather than losing coal or harming residents. The player does not gain the detailed door survey from Route B.

**[Help unload at the Ward Six delivery point]** — the cart reaches the right ward, and the fuel already on it is delivered to the ordinary receiving point. No fight or confrontational guard appears in this job; **Silas Pumpard arrives only in Stage 5's later Sealed Stove/Coal Hook sequence**.

*The receiving worker looks at the load and the slip.*

**RECEIVER:** “This won't go far.”

**PLAYER:** “It's all we were given. I counted it.”

**RECEIVER:** “Then put the shortage on the paper. Don't have them saying we lost it here.”

**[Record what actually arrived]** — record the difference between the amount ordered and the sacks actually delivered. The receiving clerk keeps the delivery record; the player copies the shortage into their own brief working note without acquiring the official slip. This doesn't supply the missing coal or let the player open the padlocked communal coal box.

**Branch finding:** **The loaders put far less coal on the Ward Six cart than the city had ordered. The player ensured the smaller shipment arrived and its shortage was recorded.** No permanent fuel supply, proof of theft or magically filled households.

## Stage 4 end: one finding, one return path

After **exactly one** of these jobs, the player continues by ordinary city movement towards their home ward. On the route they will see the locked-in-outline **Stage 5 — The Sealed Stove**, a padlocked communal coal box outside a cold tenement, and later encounter **Silas Pumpard**. Barnaby Rusk, Owler and the other backgrounds' opponents do not appear.

| Job completed | What the player knows at the start of Stage 5 | What is not solved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Coal Waiver** | The appeal was received, intentionally postponed until spring, and its delay formally disputed | The heating order still stands |
| **The Door Count** | The listed houses are occupied, one tenant burns floorboards, and a coal-cart route is marked | No new coal has been delivered through this task |
| **The Short Cart** | The delivered consignment is badly short, and the smaller load's arrival was recorded | It will not heat the ward through winter |

**[Write down what you found] → [Fold the two working notes]** is the common, short Stage 4 closing action (included in the ordinary job closeout, not a separate reward phase). One small note records the shared Ward Six loss-sheet observation from Stage 3; the second records the **one** selected branch finding. Both are visible quest props carried to Stage 5, then retained after Stage 6 regardless of victory/defeat; no permanent equipment or tradeable paper is added. The original office sheets, route map and delivery tally stay with their owners. At the sealed box the player may present only **their chosen branch's evidence**. Do not manufacture an appeal reference for the driver branch or make the Contracts player know the secret side streets.

## Common gameplay, money and safety rules

- Keep visible text understandable without a background in law, geography or industry. The player checks **papers, dates, doors, routes, sacks and deliveries**, not arcane regulations or advanced coal machinery.
- The three jobs use genuinely different existing work patterns: **Contracts = compare official claim and appeal status; Navigation = count occupied doors and find a passable path; Hauling = count, balance and deliver sacks**.
- No extra coins, ordinary XP, items, weapon, Medicine bonuses or second-skill +1. **The single +1 happened upon committing to one Stage 3 route**; save that choice and its finding against reload duplication.
- Time and recoverable mistakes follow the normal [Jobs and Time](../../jobs/jobs-and-time.md) rules. Players must see their meaningful interaction early. A wrong turn or inaccurate copied figure triggers correction, not automatic deaths or an impossible combat softlock.
- No extra named surveyor, fuel clerk, carter, receiver or resident. No new city-wide policy, secret conspiracy, procedural amnesty, landlord promise or Pall-related plot established here.
- Maintain one narrow immediate respite per route; the winter threat persists and the player's later confrontation has a grounded cause.
