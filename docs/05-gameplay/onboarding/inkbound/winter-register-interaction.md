## What the player is actually doing

**Compare two lists of addresses and household numbers. One list contains corrections. Work out which numbers to copy, fix one hard-to-read entry and hand Erwin a clean page.**

No secret code, legal puzzle, or accounting background is needed. The player is a working copyist, already granted Scholarship +3; they use pens, paper and books supplied by the office rather than buying items.

## S02-A — Open the books

**[Read the work sheet]**

> **The Winter Register — Scholarship / Investigation**  
> Compare the old winter list with its correction sheet.  
> Check the changes. Copy the right numbers on a fresh page.  
> **This internal office check pays 0 coins and 0 ordinary XP.**  
> The books, paper and ink belong to the copying office.

**[Compare the two lists]**

The player sees two short, ordinary **sample household entries** presented beside each other:

| Address shown | Earlier list | Corrected sheet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House 12 | 4 people | 4 people |
| House 14 | 6 people | 5 people |

*The second sheet has a note beside House 14: “One person moved.” It also bears the later date and an ordinary office correction mark.*

These numbers are **illustrative data for this proposed ordinary job**. They have no relation to Ward Six's later canonical loss number, and they do not define housing population across the city. The normal Investigation task compares entries, dates and visible correction marks; it does not rely on players knowing historical clerical terminology.

**[Check which sheet is newer]** — makes it clear that the House 14 entry has been legitimately corrected from **6 to 5**, while House 12 stayed the same. The player can inspect the margin note again; no guessing about what a scribble means is required.

## S02-B — How to handle the difference

After reading both versions, choose **one practical approach**:

| Player action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **[Check the note and copy the changed number]** | Take longer to confirm why House 14 changed, then copy **5** correctly |
| **[Copy from the corrected sheet]** | Faster. Because the sheet is clearly marked later, the same correct **5** is used, but the player has not personally checked why it changed |

Both paths are valid, beginner-safe **Investigation** approaches with a time-versus-verification tradeoff, not right-answer quizzes. For paid repeatable jobs, the later generic system can make source uncertainty affect error risk; here both are safe because the later date and office mark are visible. An action labeled “copy from the corrected sheet” should not secretly punish the player for trusting a legible, authorized correction.

**[Copy the household entries]** — write the two entries on clean office paper using ordinary copying interactions. The item remains a work prop, not a permanent collectible census scroll.

## S02-C — A normal mistake, not the story twist

*On the next line, a spot of ink covers part of a number. The end of the figure might be a 3 or an 8.*

**[Look at the blurred number]** — shows what is unclear. The copyist must avoid silently inventing a household count.

Choose one:

- **[Check the older book]** — compare the same address on the original list. It takes more time, but reveals the legible source. If a later correction exists, compare that note as well rather than copying an out-of-date count.
- **[Ask Erwin to check it]** — he inspects the original page and points out the readable number. It's an ordinary request of the supervising senior clerk and takes a little of his time; no specialist skill or new NPC appears.
- **[Put down your best guess]** — quicker, but the copy may be wrong. **[Read the line again]** or Erwin's normal final check catches it, allowing **[Correct the number]** before the job closes. The mistake adds ordinary rework/time, never a fatal error or instant failure of the entire tutorial.

No number for the blurred entry is canonized here; the later implementation chooses an actual legible source and must be consistent. The player is not asked to recognize a signature forged by a stranger or uncover a policy about coal in this routine work.

If the player asks Erwin to check the smudge, his attempt at polished courtesy gives way to real irritation when he sees how much work the blot creates:

**PLAYER:** “Can you read this? The ink's gone across the number.”

**ERWIN:** “Allow me.”

*He bends over the page, then holds it nearer the window.*

**ERWIN:** “Oh, for heaven's sake. They've made a proper mess of it.”

**PLAYER:** “Can't you make it out?”

**ERWIN:** “Not through that muck, I can't. Bring me the older book.”

*Erwin finds the clear entry and turns the book towards the player.*

**ERWIN:** “There. That's the number. Write it plainly, if you please. Paper isn't free.”

If the player instead chooses **[Put down your best guess]** and Erwin catches a wrong figure during the normal final check, use a **different short response** that acknowledges that actual player choice (not a generic error bark):

**ERWIN:** “Where did this number come from?”

**PLAYER:** “The ink covered it. I guessed.”

**ERWIN:** “Guessed? We are meant to be a respectable office. Damn it, get the older book. I'm not answering for a number you made up.”

**[Correct the number]** returns the page to the ordinary proofread flow. No extra wage or punishment, just rework. If the player reads the source correctly alone, neither exchange fires; no forced conversation.

The clipped **“Allow me”** and **“if you please”** are the voice Erwin wants; **“muck,” “proper mess,” “Damn it”** and the fretting over wasted paper are the voice he actually has when work goes wrong. Do not give him a fake accent or a constant stream of aristocratic greetings.

## S02-D — Finish the normal work

**[Check the clean copy]** — a readable line-by-line check shows whether the corrected count and smudged entry were copied accurately. If not, the player **[Correct the marked line]** before submitting.

**[Put the books back] → [Give Erwin the copy]**

*Erwin reads down the clean page. The corrected household count is marked, and the blurred line has been checked against the older book. He straightens the finished copy on his desk.*

**ERWIN:** “There. A page fit for a gentleman's desk.”

**PLAYER:** “It's a list of houses.”

**ERWIN:** “And if a number's wrong, we'll do it all again. I'd sooner not.”

*He pushes the older books towards the player.*

**ERWIN:** “Put them back, carefully. That cover's nearly worn through, and we've no money for another.”

**[Finish the work]** — closes the normal **The Winter Register** job. This exceptional office correction round gives **0 coins and 0 ordinary Scholarship XP** as advertised before entry. The player keeps their original 20 coins, Basic Clothes, Crude Small Bag and Scholarship +3. No ordinary copying task is being disguised as a quest reward.

Normal paid jobs of this work family still award coins and Scholarship XP. Errors are discovered through visible records and corrected through simple, reusable source-comparison actions. The current sample entries are not introduced as new tradable documents or permanent world facts.

## S02-E — Stage 3 must wait until the work is finished

Only **after** the clean copy is checked, delivered and the ordinary job closed does the one-time **The Acceptable Losses** discovery occur.

In Stage 3, a **separate supplementary sheet** is found tucked among the winter records. It concerns how much coal poorer households receive and contains the already locked **EXPECTED WINTER LOSSES → AUTHORIZED** change and the Ward Six loss figure.

This is **not** visible during Stage 1 or 2. No early player option mentions coal rations, dead residents, expected deaths, Ward Six, special budget columns or Silas Pumpard. The short household-copying job must be completed and understandable on its own before the grim discovery.

The original records stay at the Civic Copying Office. The later question is **what to do after seeing the extra page**; it does not retroactively make the first two pages sinister.

## Gameplay/system rules

- Reusable work sequence: **read work slip → compare two lists → check the corrected entry → copy → handle a blurred number → proofread → hand in**. Ordinary Investigation skills concern checking whether records agree, not decoding ciphers or searching for a master criminal.
- User-facing actions say **list, page, number, note, check, copy, put back**. Don't force the player to understand *reconciliation, roster amendments, transcription defects* or *source provenance*.
- Normal timing applies: required interactive work begins within the first ~2 minutes; world time continues; no forced late timer click or repeated waiting for an idle job.
- Simple, recoverable errors affect time/copy accuracy and may require a corrected page. Do not turn a smudge into a forged government seal, a crime flag, catastrophic budget mistake or unbeatable skill test.
- No free quill, paper, scroll, census ledger or special scholarship equipment is added to the starter kit; job props stay in the office.
- This tutorial occurrence pays **0 coins, 0 ordinary XP, 0 loot**. The **+1 second Major Skill** will be granted once at the player's Stage 3 branch commitment, **not here**. Scholarship +3 is already the starting background bonus.
- The separate Stage 3 event triggers only once per player, never automatically on every future Winter Register job.
