## I05-A — The Sealed Stove

**Entry:** After completing precisely one of Stage 4's unpaid tasks, the Inkbound walks through Ward Six on the usual way towards home. The ward is cold. Outside a tenement is an old shared coal stove and its separate **padlocked box of communal coal**, under the district fuel office's seal. A few residents wait by it. No one has broken into it; the inkbound holds no key.

The stove, box and padlock must be easy to recognize without knowledge of boilers or heating systems: **there is coal under lock, and the people nearby are cold**. The old fuel limit is still on the books.

*One resident looks at the papers the Inkbound carries.*

**RESIDENT:** “You're from the counting office, aren't you?”

**PLAYER:** “I work there, yes.”

**RESIDENT:** “Come to count the living, have you? Or the dead?”

**PLAYER:** “No. I saw they've ordered less coal for this street. I came to see how you're managing.”

**RESIDENT:** “Managing? We can't even keep that stove lit. Look at the lock.”

The resident has not read the private mortality sheet and should not mysteriously quote its nine-loss figure. The Inkbound has not come with legal authority to cut the lock.

**[Look at the locked coal box]** — confirms that the padlock and official seal are intact. The stove is cold; there is no authorized fuel release posted here. This is **inspection only**, not a prompt to steal fuel, smash the lock or invent a lock-picking system.

**[Tell the resident what you found]** — exactly one branch-specific exchange:

### A — The Coal Waiver (Commerce / Contracts)

*The Inkbound can show the resident the appeal entry or read its reference from the work record. No new collectible paper is issued.*

**PLAYER:** “The office has your appeal. They won't look at it until spring.”

**RESIDENT:** “Spring? We asked for heat this winter.”

**PLAYER:** “I've put the delay down in writing. Here's the number they gave it.”

**RESIDENT:** “A number, then. Will a number open that lock?”

**PLAYER:** “No. But they can't say the letter was never brought in.”

**Immediate result:** the resident knows the **specific appeal reference** and that the delay is disputed, not resolved. The padlock stays shut; nothing about the objection overrules an allocation or produces coal.

### B — The Door Count (Fieldcraft / Navigation)

**PLAYER:** “They've got houses marked empty that are full of people. I went and checked.”

**RESIDENT:** “We've told them often enough.”

**PLAYER:** “The yard entrance is blocked with rubbish. I found another street a coal cart can use to reach these houses.”

**RESIDENT:** “Then show us. The last cart never came near our door.”

**PLAYER:** “Come along. I'll point it out.”

**Immediate result:** in a **short ordinary route-view action**, **[Point out the open street]**, the player indicates the passable delivery path to the resident. This is information, not a summoned shipment. The player already marked the route in approved Stage 4. The same houses remain cold; the fuel box remains sealed.

### C — The Short Cart (Industry / Hauling)

**PLAYER:** “I helped with the coal cart. They didn't load nearly as much as they were meant to.”

**RESIDENT:** “But it came?”

**PLAYER:** “Yes. We delivered the sacks we had. They didn't give us any more.”

**RESIDENT:** “Then where's ours?”

**PLAYER:** “I don't know. We wrote down what arrived.”

**Immediate result:** the resident learns **a smaller delivery did reach Ward Six's receiving point and the shortage was recorded**. Do not retroactively place the whole load in this particular communal coal box, unlock it, or imply delivery is the same as residents receiving their rations. No one knows who removed or withheld the missing sacks.

### All three routes converge

*The resident looks from the Inkbound to the locked box.*

**RESIDENT:** “That'll be a fine comfort tonight.”

There is anger and exhaustion, not a dramatic speech. The ward has gained **a point of leverage or useful information**, but no winter's worth of fuel and no promise of permanent relief.

**[Look at the office seal]** — the seal is still in place. No route has gained authority to break it. The Inkbound notices a watchman's rounds marked on the box or hears his steps coming along the street. This is a normal guard arrival, **not evidence of a coordinated secret plot**.

## I05-B — The Coal Hook

*Silas Pumpard, the contracted Fuel Watchman, comes down the street with a short coal hook used for handling sacks. He looks first at the people gathered round the box, then at the Inkbound's papers.*

**SILAS:** “What's all this? Get away from the coal.”

**PLAYER:** “Nobody's touched it.”

**SILAS:** “And nobody will. It's under my charge.”

**RESIDENT:** “Then let us have some. The stove's gone cold.”

**SILAS:** “Not without an order. You know that.”

*He points his hook at the Inkbound's papers.*

**SILAS:** “What have you brought them?”

**PLAYER:** “What I found in the books. The coal order isn't right.”

**SILAS:** “Give me that paper.”

**PLAYER:** “No.”

**SILAS:** “Then take yourself off. I'll not have the whole street gathering round my box.”

His concern is his paid responsibility for **the locked allotment and the official seal**; he fears being held responsible if coal goes missing. He is not an officer of the police, a murderous zealot, a city budget author or an accomplice to Erwin. His hook is a plausible threatening **work implement**, not a special loot item or guaranteed final weapon choice; precise combat implement and damage remain TBD.

**No route creates a new official order for Silas:** the appeal remains pending, the surveyed route concerns travel, and the coal-cart record confirms a short load without authorizing release from this box. Accordingly he does **not** hand out coal on one skill branch and attack on another. The player's chosen evidence changes what the residents know, not his motive to enforce the seal.

### Choose one approach before the fight

| Player-facing response | What actually changes |
| --- | --- |
| **[Stand your ground]** | The player stands between Silas and the resident, out in the open with little space between them |
| **[Step behind the coal box]** | The solid box stands to one side of the player, forcing Silas to come around it rather than simply charge in a straight line |
| **[Tell him to leave the papers alone]** | The player stays a little farther back and tries words once more; there is room to move but no ready barrier |

If the third choice is selected:

**PLAYER:** “It's the council's writing. Take your quarrel to them.”

**SILAS:** “You're the one waving it about. Hand it over.”

For either physical positioning choice, he instead says:

**SILAS:** “I said give it here.”

*Silas steps towards the player, hook in hand, and grabs for the papers. The Inkbound refuses to hand them over. Silas raises his free arm to strike.*

**Stage 6 begins now**, as a real short turn-based PvE confrontation. The resident and other nearby witnesses remain in the street, outside the fight. The lock and seal are **intact before combat**, with nobody opening the box in a cutscene while the player has no control.

## Gameplay and continuity

- Stage 5 has **two distinct interactions**: inspect the fuel box and speak with the resident; then refuse Silas's paper demand and choose a position. No free item, lock-picking action, moral slider or custom minigame.
- **One branch only:** know the appeal number (Commerce) **or** the passable route (Fieldcraft) **or** the delivered-but-short consignment (Industry). Do not manufacture the other two findings. The overarching Ward Six loss-sheet discovery belongs to all routes and remains recorded.
- “Papers” are the player's ordinary work slips and recorded findings, **not irreplaceable physical loot to seize**. Silas has no authority to delete the player's earlier finding from the journal. Whether a branch paper is displayed physically is visual scripting TBD.
- The padlocked communal box and stove are nearby props and readable obstacles. They **must not spontaneously supply fuel**, become a personal storage chest, or act as an automatic combat victory.
