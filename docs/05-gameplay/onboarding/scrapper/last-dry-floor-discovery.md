## What the player discovers

**When:** The first pump-cleanup job is finished. The two old outer metal plates have been removed and sorted, the tools returned, and Ignatius has checked the work. **Only then** the one-time story event begins. This is not a repeatable result from every normal Mechanics job.

*Walking past the pump again, the Scrapper notices the needle moving behind a round glass dial. It rises, falls, then rises again.*

**[Look at the dial]** — even though the pump was marked for scrap, **it is still running and moving water**. The player is not asked to understand pressure engineering or operate the machine.

*A strip of cloth is caught beside a narrow pipe that leads down beneath the iron floor.*

**[Read the cloth]**

> **DON'T STOP IT. WATER REACHES THE BEDS.**

*Three knocks sound from under the floor. There is a pause, then three more.*

**[Knock back]** — the player taps the iron floor. Someone answers from beneath it.

**VOICE BELOW:** “Is somebody up there? Don't turn that pump off. We're living down here.”

**PLAYER:** “How many of you?”

**VOICE BELOW:** “More than one family. The rooms fill with water when it stops.”

The player can hear someone living below, but has not explored the cellar, learned their names, counted exact occupants or proven every detail of their tenancy. This is a threatened ordinary human settlement, **not a supernatural event**. Do not add a child in danger, a named quest-giver or a revealed mastermind as an extra shock.

The story's implication is legible even to a player who knows nothing of machines: **this supposedly unwanted pump is keeping water out of people's rooms. Cutting its main pipe now could flood them**. Stage 2 explicitly left that pipe alone.

## Ask Ignatius

**[Tell Smog]** — the player shows him the warning and indicates the knocking under the floor.

*Ignatius coughs, then looks from the cloth to the moving needle.*

**PLAYER:** “There's people living under us. This pump's keeping the water out.”

**SMOG:** “Under the yard?”

**PLAYER:** “I heard them. And look, it's still running.”

**SMOG:** “The papers say it's dead. That's what they sent me.”

**PLAYER:** “Well, they're wrong.”

**SMOG:** “I can see that.”

He knows what the yard's paperwork says, not who secretly occupies the old cellar rooms. He isn't established as knowing a plan to hurt them. His small admission is deliberately plain; no fresh-air catchphrase here, no speech about corruption and no sudden solution from an all-knowing foreman.

**[Look at the work order]** — the same yard paperwork calls the pump **“abandoned”** and lists the **main copper pipe** for later cutting and sale. This is not a separate player command to cut it. The immediate decision is how to buy time **before** another scrap worker gets to that pipe.

If the player first checks the old order or first knocks back, the same grounded evidence converges. The facts are never hidden behind a Mechanics skill test.

## Three player choices

Each option opens a *different, ordinary job activity* in [approved Stage 4](second-task-branches.md). All three temporarily help the people below in a distinct way. None fixes their housing problem forever.

| Player choice (button text) | What the character will do | Second Major Skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Hold the water back]** | Join a drain crew to put temporary supports by the leaking wall while the pump keeps running | **Industry +1** — Construction, *The Brace Shift* |
| **[Find a way out]** | Check a dry side passage and mark a path the residents can follow if water comes in | **Fieldcraft +1** — Navigation, *The Low Passage* |
| **[Find another way to drain the water]** | Ask the yard office to send a spare drainage hose so the old pipe needn't be the only protection | **Commerce +1** — Logistics, *The Spare Line* |

**Exact short player speech, exclusive to the choice:**

- **Hold the water back — PLAYER:** “I'll see if the drain men can shore up that wall.”
- **Find a way out — PLAYER:** “They need a way out before anyone cuts that pipe.”
- **Find another way to drain the water — PLAYER:** “I'll ask the yard if there's another hose we can use.”

**SMOG (same answer for all three):** “Go on, then. I'll leave the pipe be for now.”

This is a **temporary personal decision by Ignatius** about his own yard work, not a promise that every other piece-rate worker obeys him or that the city has changed the salvage order. It creates the later conflict with Barnaby Rusk, who expects to be paid for cutting copper.

## When the skill bonus happens

At the instant the player commits to **one** choice, award **exactly +1 Major Skill level** in **Industry, Fieldcraft or Commerce**, respectively. The original **Mechanics +3** at character creation stays unchanged. The selection is stored; switching later or reloading cannot claim the other bonuses. Stage 4 will not grant the +1 again.

This is a **one-time exceptional follow-up**, not an accepted paid contract: the player has chosen to respond to an emergency rather than taken an ordinary customer-paid shift. Stage 4 earns **0 coins, 0 ordinary XP and no loot**. Outside onboarding, Construction, Navigation and Logistics jobs of the same kinds remain ordinarily paid.

No hidden moral rating, theft flag, permanent property title, new supernatural clue or start of the game's main plot. The resident warning can be saved as a quest finding, not a sellable cloth item.

## Limits and links

- No player dismantles the large pipe or turns the pump off during the event.
- “Abandoned” is what the **yard's work order claims**, not the narrator's truth. The city can believe rooms are empty while actual families live there.
- Ordinary staff are allowed to **not know** the entire story. Smog has only the evidence he sees and the official instructions he was given.
- The player does not yet learn that the wall is cracked (Construction), the dry escape passage's condition (Navigation), or the spare-hose stock and funding dispute (Logistics). Those are **exclusive Stage 4 findings**.
- The three deliberate knocks remain the established sign of people below, not a code, puzzle lock or timer to solve.
- The next antagonist, **Barnaby Rusk**, appears only **after Stage 4**, in the already locked-in-outline *Copper Claim / Red Lever* sequence.
