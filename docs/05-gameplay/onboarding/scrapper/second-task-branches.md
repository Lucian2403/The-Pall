## A. The Brace Shift — Industry / Construction

**Player chose [Hold the water back].** Join a small city drain crew working beside Rust Yard. The aim is not to rebuild a whole wall or fix the pump. It is to **stop water getting through a damaged stretch of wall for a little while**, so the people below have time.

### Ordinary work the player can understand

1. **[Look at the leaking wall]** — with the unnamed crew lead, see a crack near its base and water coming through. This is the **branch's new fact**, not something revealed in Stage 3.
2. **[Choose where to set the boards]** — the player sees that the ground beside one part of the wall is soft, while a nearby patch is firmer:
   - **[Put the boards on firm ground]** — takes more carrying, but gives the temporary support a steadier footing.
   - **[Start beside the crack]** — shorter carry, but requires **[Clear and firm the ground]** before the boards will sit properly. The player can correct it; no instant wall collapse.
3. **[Help the crew place the boards and sandbags]** — the workers supervise the temporary support. Ordinary Construction work teaches **site inspection, preparation, carrying/placing and checking**; it is **not** a disguised Mechanics pump repair.
4. **[Check where the water goes]** — the crew checks that the supports have slowed the leak without trapping more water against the wall. If the ground or placement needs correcting, show an ordinary fix before sign-off.

*The crew lead puts a hand against the damp wall.*

**CREW LEAD:** “There's a crack down here. Water's getting through.”

**PLAYER:** “Will these boards hold it?”

**CREW LEAD:** “For now. This wall needs proper work.”

**PLAYER:** “And the people below?”

**CREW LEAD:** “They've got a bit of time. That's all.”

**[Finish the drain work]** — save the fact: **the wall is cracked and temporary supports buy time; they do not make the occupied rooms permanently safe**. The crew keeps its boards, tools and work record. No free materials, wages or XP for the tutorial. Later regular Construction jobs are paid.

## B. The Low Passage — Fieldcraft / Navigation

**Player chose [Find a way out].** Follow an existing **side passage beside the old drain** with an unnamed route worker. Check whether people can walk from the rooms beneath Rust Yard to a safe exit. This is pathfinding, not repair work or a dungeon battle.

### Ordinary route work

1. **[Look over the passage]** — view two accessible ways round a low stretch of the drain. The player can see where the floor is dry and where water has made it slippery. No specialist map reading is needed to understand the difference.
2. **[Choose which way to check]**:
   - **[Try the short way]** — fewer turns, but debris blocks a tight part. **[Mark the blockage and go round]** is allowed, preserving a truthful route instead of demanding a dangerous crawl.
   - **[Take the dry side path]** — a longer walk with clear footing, fewer obstructions.
3. **[Mark the turns]** — with **yard/route-worker chalk**, mark the safe bends and a clear direction back out. Chalk is a work prop, not a player inventory reward.
4. **[Walk back along the marked way]** — confirm someone can follow the markings **both out and back**. The player does not automatically move every resident, escort a helpless child, or assume a corridor remains dry forever.

*Near the lower rooms, an unnamed resident speaks from the doorway.*

**RESIDENT:** “Does it come out above ground?”

**PLAYER:** “Yes. I marked the turns. You can follow them.”

**RESIDENT:** “I'll tell the others. Some won't leave their things.”

**PLAYER:** “The pump could stop. They need to know.”

**[Finish checking the way out]** — save the fact: **a usable dry side path has been marked, but the residents have not all agreed to leave**. No automatic city housing, new named family, secret tunnel destination or guaranteed mass evacuation. The route worker keeps the chalk and route notes; no money/XP in this emergency instance. Normal Navigation surveying later pays.

## C. The Spare Line — Commerce / Logistics

**Player chose [Find another way to drain the water].** At Rust Yard's regular dispatch desk, help an unnamed clerk arrange for **a spare hose to reach the threatened pump**, so the old copper pipe is not the residents' only hope. This is an ordering and delivery job, not a Mechanics repair.

### Ordinary dispatch work

1. **[Look at the yard's spare parts list]** — see that the yard has a suitable **spare drainage hose set aside for emergency work**. The city has marked the place below Rust Yard as empty, so a normal paid delivery has **no authorized customer or budget**.
2. **[Choose where it needs to go]** — compare two slips: one lists “Rust Yard” in general; the other points to the **pump by the drain**. The player must select the latter so the hose reaches the right place. This is an ordinary destination check, not an obscure mechanical test.
3. **[Check what the clerk can send]** — the clerk may put the spare hose on a **temporary emergency request**, but cannot promise the city will pay for it or agree to replace the pump for good. Show the difference between **send a spare now** and **approve a permanent job**.
4. **[Send the hose with a yard worker]** — arrange movement through the ordinary Logistics job. The worker keeps the hose as **yard-owned equipment**; it is brought to the pump for the repair crew to check and fit, **not connected by the player** or treated as instantly safe. The working old pump stays on.

*At the dispatch desk, the clerk looks from the order book to the player.*

**CLERK:** “They've got the place down as empty.”

**PLAYER:** “It isn't. There's people living underneath.”

**CLERK:** “Then who's paying for the hose?”

**PLAYER:** “Can't we send the spare one?”

**CLERK:** “For now, yes. I'll have to put the cost somewhere.”

**[Finish the delivery slip]** — save the fact: **a temporary spare hose is on its way or staged at the right pump, but no one has agreed to pay for lasting drainage work**. The job does **not** certify the new line as installed, make the old pipe safe to cut, or turn the player into an engineer. Exact fitting steps belong to the eventual normal machinery/crew systems, not this Logistics tutorial. The employer owns all goods. No coins/XP.

## One common return to Rust Yard

At the end of **exactly one** branch, the player returns by ordinary travel to the yard where the old pump is still running. The main pipe has not been cut. The player carries **one specific piece of evidence or practical help** into the already locked-in-outline Stage 5, *The Copper Claim*:

| Chosen job | What the player can accurately tell Barnaby |
| --- | --- |
| **The Brace Shift** | “There's water coming through the wall. The drain men have propped it up, but it won't last.” |
| **The Low Passage** | “I found a way out and marked it. The people below still need time.” |
| **The Spare Line** | “We've sent a spare hose to the pump. Nobody's connected it yet.” |

**These lines are proposed Stage 5 continuity facts**, not newly approved spoken confrontation dialogue. Do not put them into Stage 4 as speech to Barnaby; he appears only in the next stage. In Stage 5 he is a fellow scrap worker expecting a paid copper-cutting job, not a hidden mastermind.

The Stage 4 ending does not claim that **temporary boards**, **a marked way out** or **a hose waiting to be fitted** are permanent solutions. The household danger remains real, but **all three routes grant a plausible chance to delay the cut**. The exact response affects the later argument, not whether the tutorial can finish after the fight.

- The chosen **+1 Industry / Fieldcraft / Commerce** was given exactly once at Stage 3 branch selection. Here **0 coins, 0 ordinary XP**, and no additional +1. Later non-tutorial paid work remains paid and earns XP.
- A route reveals **only its own** special fact. Every route already knows from Stage 3 that the pump is running, the cloth warns of rising water and people are below. It does not automatically know the other two routes' findings.
