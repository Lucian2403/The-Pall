## First paid job: The Winter Register — Scholarship / Investigation (LOCKED)

A routine copy-and-verification assignment. The player checks ward rosters, reconciles household counts, distinguishes original from amended totals and flags mismatched column entries. This is normal, reusable **Investigation** work, not a tutorial-exclusive puzzle.

Generic phases: accept terms/pay/time; compare two rolls, select the correct source, decide which discrepancy to investigate; resolve a normal missing-seal or transcription complication; submit accepted work for regular Scholarship XP and coins. The narrative twist triggers **only after those generic interactions**. The original personal record numbers and coal allocation remain task/quest props, not free permanent inventory items.

## The twist — The Acceptable Losses (LOCKED)

Tucked inside the ordinary winter census is a supplementary column, not meant to be copied for the public roll. It lists a row of overcrowded tenements, their coal allotment, and an estimate of how many inhabitants may die if the heat is cut back.

The printed heading is **EXPECTED WINTER LOSSES**. An older, more deliberate hand has struck through *expected* and written **AUTHORIZED**.

> WARD SIX — REDUCED HEAT / ACCEPTABLE LOSSES: NINE.

This isn't a supernatural prophecy or a main-story mystery. The underlying horror is administrative: officials are using a predicted death toll as an acceptable consequence of cutting fuel and food spending. **Whether this practice is systemic or the work of one ruthless official is not established by this introductory episode.** The specific Ward Six / nine-loss figure is an **approved scripted incident detail**, not an established population or citywide mortality rule.

Erwin Buro urges the player to finish the copy. He knows too much about what the column means, but not necessarily who approved it.

## Two follow-up interactions and common enemy (LOCKED narrative)

**Interaction 1 — The Sealed Stove:** On the route toward home, the Inkbound sees a padlocked communal coal box outside a cold tenement. A resident asks whether the census writer has come to count the living or the dead. The player may show their branch's docket, inspect the sealed box or identify the shortage. Each route yields different intelligible dialogue and a small immediate consequence: an appeal number, a safe access route or one arriving cart. None magically replaces an entire winter ration.

**Interaction 2 — The Coal Hook:** The district's contracted fuel watchman, **Silas Pumpard** (exact personal name LOCKED), arrives to protect the fuel allotment and the seal. He is paid to enforce a ledger and does not want a copying-room worker inviting a crowd. He tries to seize the paper or turn the player away; physical intimidation escalates to the required short **turn-based PvE** confrontation. Player can stand ground, step behind the coal box or use the surroundings defensively. Detailed combat actions and stats remain deferred to the global combat design.

**LOCKED enemy:** **Silas Pumpard, Fuel Watchman**, a minor contracted guard, not a police commander or supernatural being. His motivation is his earnings, the locked fuel box and fear of being punished for breaking his orders. A credible low-tier opponent for an unarmed, fresh character: win by driving him away / making him abandon the confrontation rather than forcing a killing. Fight must be moderately challenging but recoverable even after defeat. Exact weapon, AI, injury and loot TBD.

## Healing and the last page

After the fight the Inkbound is visibly injured and must use the regular paid clinical treatment process, spending a meaningful but affordable amount. They return to their own cramped lodging with little money. The same bureaucracy that calculated *acceptable losses* has left a **rent-due notice** on their door.

The episode ends. The ward has obtained a small practical reprieve according to the chosen route; the mortality allowance isn't overturned by one junior scribe, and nothing about this quest starts the overarching main storyline.
