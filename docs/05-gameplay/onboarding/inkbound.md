# Inkbound — The Acceptable Losses

**Status: PROPOSAL, not user-approved (2026-10-13).** This designs the sixth/final opening background for review. **Scholarship +3**, the same starting kit as every origin, unique first-job twist → three choice-dependent second-Major-Skill jobs → two more interactions → turn-based PvE → paid treatment → home/rent are already LOCKED globally in [Shared Tutorial Structure](shared-structure.md). No new locations, personal names, script elements, rewards, or wider municipal practices in this file are canon until approved.

## Proposed hub and recurring NPC

**Civic Copying Office** — a publicly contracted counting-room near Grand Cylinder City's poorer wards: ink-dark windows, sand used to dry fresh writing, scorched tally slips, wooden record bins and clerks transcribing household rolls and industry reports. This is a **permanent Scholarship / Investigation job hub**, not a tutorial-only institution.

**Proposed regular job NPC:** **Edwin Vale, Senior Copy Clerk** (personal name and title both proposals). He hires ordinary copyists, checks their accuracy and pays normal Scholarship coins/XP. His attitude is pragmatic, not obviously villainous; he worries about losing the municipal account that keeps his clerks employed. Every NPC ultimately needs a personal name, including the street surveyor, coal carrier and opponent, but names aren't fixed merely by introducing this pitch.

The Inkbound character already works in the copying trade. Character creation grants **Scholarship +3** to its Major Skill and normal STR/DEX/INT/RES 5, 50 max Health, **Basic Clothes + Crude Small Bag + 20 coins**.

## First paid job: The Winter Register — Scholarship / Investigation (PROPOSED)

A routine copy-and-verification assignment. The player checks ward rosters, reconciles household counts, distinguishes original from amended totals and flags mismatched column entries. This is normal, reusable **Investigation** work, not a tutorial-exclusive puzzle.

Generic phases: accept terms/pay/time; compare two rolls, select the correct source, decide which discrepancy to investigate; resolve a normal missing-seal or transcription complication; submit accepted work for regular Scholarship XP and coins. The narrative twist triggers **only after those generic interactions**. The original personal record numbers and coal allocation remain task/quest props, not free permanent inventory items.

## The twist — The Acceptable Losses (PROPOSED)

Tucked inside the ordinary winter census is a supplementary column, not meant to be copied for the public roll. It lists a row of overcrowded tenements, their coal allotment, and an estimate of how many inhabitants may die if the heat is cut back.

The printed heading is **EXPECTED WINTER LOSSES**. An older, more deliberate hand has struck through *expected* and written **AUTHORIZED**.

> WARD SIX — REDUCED HEAT / ACCEPTABLE LOSSES: NINE.

This isn't a supernatural prophecy or a main-story mystery. The underlying horror is administrative: officials are using a predicted death toll as an acceptable consequence of cutting fuel and food spending. **Whether this practice is systemic or the work of one ruthless official is not established by this introductory episode.** The number nine and Ward Six are *proposal script props* and aren't tied to locked city demographics.

Edwin Vale urges the player to finish the copy. He knows too much about what the column means, but not necessarily who approved it.

| **Challenge the calculation** — prove a budget line is being treated as legal authority | **Commerce → Contracts: The Coal Waiver.** Take ordinary paid document-reconciliation work at a fuel-allocation counter; examine signatures, approval clauses and competing claims. | The ward's heating appeal is marked *received* but intentionally not processed until spring; the player secures a copy/temporary dispute note but no automatic citywide reversal. |
| **Count the people, not the ledger** — verify that the affected households are still occupied | **Fieldcraft → Navigation: The Door Count.** Join a paid low-tier ward/route survey for safe delivery access, mapping doorways and checking blocked courtyards with ordinary route and observation interactions. | The supposed empty tenements are full. | **Follow the missing coal** — discover where the reduced ration is being held | **Industry → Hauling: The Short Cart.** Earn ordinary payment for moving/inspecting a small municipal fuel consignment, choosing load and delivery route and resolving a manifest discrepancy. | The contracted cart has far less coal than the route requires. All three second jobs have genuine normal gameplay and pay normal wages/XP for **Commerce, Fieldcraft or Industry**, respectively. They don't make the player powerful enough to solve an entire city's heating problem, and no special starting items or second Major Skill bonuses are implied.

## Two follow-up interactions and common enemy (PROPOSED)

**Interaction 1 — The Sealed Stove:** On the route toward home, the Inkbound sees a padlocked communal coal box outside a cold tenement. A resident asks whether the census writer has come to count the living or the dead. The player may show their branch's docket, inspect the sealed box or identify the shortage. Each route yields different intelligible dialogue and a small immediate consequence: an appeal number, a safe access route or one arriving cart. None magically replaces an entire winter ration.

**Interaction 2 — The Coal Hook:** The district's contracted fuel watchman, **Silas Pritchard** (proposed personal name), arrives to protect the fuel allotment and the seal. He is paid to enforce a ledger and does not want a copying-room worker inviting a crowd. He tries to seize the paper or turn the player away; physical intimidation escalates to the required short **turn-based PvE** confrontation. Player can stand ground, step behind the coal box or use the surroundings defensively. Detailed combat actions and stats remain deferred to the global combat design.

**Proposed enemy:** **Silas Pritchard, Fuel Watchman**, a minor contracted guard, not a police commander or supernatural being. His motivation is his earnings, the locked fuel box and fear of being punished for breaking his orders. A credible low-tier opponent for an unarmed, fresh character: win by driving him away / making him abandon the confrontation rather than forcing a killing. Fight must be moderately challenging but recoverable even after defeat. Exact weapon, AI, injury and loot TBD.

## Healing and the last page

After the fight the Inkbound is visibly injured and must use the regular paid clinical treatment process, spending a meaningful but affordable amount. They return to their own cramped lodging with little money. The same bureaucracy that calculated *acceptable losses* has left a **rent-due notice** on their door.

The episode ends. The ward has obtained a small practical reprieve according to the chosen route; the mortality allowance isn't overturned by one junior scribe, and nothing about this quest starts the overarching main storyline.
