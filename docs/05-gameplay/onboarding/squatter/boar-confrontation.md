# Squatter — Stage 5: The Rustling Bank / Nowhere to Step (PROPOSED)

**Status: PROPOSED FOR USER APPROVAL (2026-10-15); NOT LOCKED.** The names and sequence of these **two** pre-fight interactions, and the **young injured wild boar** as the opponent, are already established in the [Squatter master](README.md). The observation choices, environmental staging and tactical starting positions below are proposed. Stage 6 will design the real turn-based animal fight and its consequences.

The player has finished exactly one of the [three Stage 4 second-skill activities](second-task-branches.md). The public approach road to Grand Cylinder City's gate runs near the disturbed drainage bank. No one has summoned a boar, and the animal has no knowledge of the player's paperwork or earlier branch. It is a wild creature reacting to noise, pain and restricted space—not a malicious or supernatural enemy.

## Interaction 1 — The Rustling Bank

*On the public path, reeds shift by a patch of broken soil. Something scrapes against the bank. A wet clump of earth falls into the ditch.*

The animal itself is still obscured. The player is in the **protected outskirts**, outside any danger-zone expedition. The evidence can be checked from the path without walking into contaminated water or digging around the poorhouse remains.

**[Inspect the tracks]** — look at visible split-hoof prints and a staggered sequence of steps. The tracks suggest a hoofed animal moving awkwardly; with starting Fieldcraft familiarity, the player can reasonably suspect a boar, but the **injury's cause and exact condition remain unknown**. The player gains early awareness of the likely animal threat, not a supernatural tracking bonus.

**[Take the raised path]** — move a little higher along the bank to avoid crowding whatever is in the reeds. The player gets more room and a better approach angle, but doesn't see the hoof marks as clearly.

**[Look at the fresh tear in the ground]** — inspect the newly collapsed edge from the safe side and notice that the direct downward route into the reeds is unstable. This warns against charging straight through a mud bank, without identifying why it collapsed.

All three choices advance. None guarantees the boar will disappear, forces the protagonist to step into danger, awards equipment, or creates new Tracking skill ranks. Record the choice as one sensible **approach-state** for the upcoming encounter; its eventual combat translation remains TBD.

**Proposed short player aside, only after seeing the hoof marks:**

**PLAYER:** “Something's been limping through here.”

A Squatter can recognize signs of an animal's awkward gait without diagnosing a specific wound. No NPC appears to explain the tracks.

## Interaction 2 — Nowhere to Step

*The reeds break apart. A young wild boar scrambles out onto the narrow path. It favours one leg and flinches when it puts weight on it. Mud covers its flank.*

*It turns toward the open bank, but the loose ground falls away under its hooves. Behind it lies the tangle of reeds and the partly collapsed drainage edge. The player is between the frightened animal and the firmer way ahead.*

This explains the confrontation without inventing a trained attack animal, magical rage or a boar that pursues people for revenge: **the animal is injured, startled and trying to reach solid ground**. It may still charge when it cannot get past safely. The Squatter cannot be ordered to help it, touch it or kill it.

**PLAYER (proposed):** “Easy. I'm not coming after you.”

*The boar stamps once and swings its head toward the path. It is too close to slip past without risking a charge.*

### Choose a physical approach

| Approved-in-principle option from the master | What the player actually does | Possible Stage 6 entry consequence — no numeric commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Hold your ground]** | Face the boar from the firmer centre of the narrow path and prepare to protect yourself | Defensive proximity; less distance if it charges |
| **[Move behind the broken fence]** | Step behind the nearby damaged fence section, putting an obstruction between the player and the animal | A physical barrier that may slow or deflect a rush; fence is not an invulnerable safe room |
| **[Move toward the stone embankment]** | Shift toward the more solid raised stonework to gain room away from loose mud | Slight change of height/spacing, with limited room to turn |

These options do not mean the player has chosen to attack the animal. A real short **turn-based PvE** encounter follows all three. Later combat can resolve by **driving the boar away, creating a passage or getting clear**; nothing in Stage 5 requires a kill, a bare-handed wrestling match or a forced medical procedure on the animal. Actual enemy behaviour, initiative, available unarmed actions and defensive effectiveness belong to Stage 6 and the general combat system.

### The player's values are not replaced by Benny's

Benny loves plants and pities vulnerable animals, including those in danger zones. His belief should remain consistent if he later learns of the boar. But **he is not present at this encounter** and his compassion does not remove the real risk of an injured wild animal. The Squatter may reasonably be frightened or protective of themself. Don't write a compulsory “save the animal” speech or turn Benny's viewpoint into a scripted character morality choice.

## Continuity and implementation

- Stage 5 only activates after one selected Stage 4 session is marked done. All branches reconnect via ordinary movement to the same bank; the boar reacts to the scene, not to which skill route was selected.
- Track the selected Stage 4 fact and the approach-state chosen in **The Rustling Bank** separately from the three defensive stances in **Nowhere to Step**. The global combat system will decide how each can matter mechanically; don't secretly add numeric bonuses now.
- Do not put medical linens, human remains or suspected dirty cuttings in the player's combat inventory. The boar is **not loot**, and its injury is not proof that the drainage issue or Pall caused it.
- If the player had told Benny about the patch, he still cannot spontaneously arrive at the bank. If the player said nothing, neither the boar nor witnesses know the concealed discovery.
- Avoid teleporting the player back to the bank from Stage 4; exact service-point geometry is TBD, but all route exits must have a natural public approach past the outfall edge.
- No extra formal decision is needed between the Stage 5 second stance and starting the Stage 6 fight. The episode's usual clinic/home continuation follows the fight, not this scene.
