## Scene S01 — A routine herb order (APPROVED)

*At the Hedge Shelter, Benny Ben sorts a bundle of yarrow. He straightens a bent stem before setting it on the scale.*

**BENNY:** “Got a yarrow order today. There's a good patch along the marked path. Just don't strip it bare.”

**PLAYER (optional):** “You worry more about the patch than the order.”

**BENNY (optional reply):** “I worry about both. Look after the plants, and there's medicine to sell and work to be had next season. Pull them all up now, and what are we left with?”

**PLAYER (optional follow-up):** “You worry about the plants out in the danger zones too?”

**BENNY (optional follow-up reply):** “Of course I do. And the animals. Poor things, out there with nobody to look after them. I can't do much for those, but we can take care of what's left here.”

**BENNY (convergence, including if the player skips the optional exchange):** “Take the good stems. Leave the roots and some plants standing. The amount's on the board. Bring the cuttings back here.”

**[Check the order board]** → inspect the assigned Yarrow quantity, nearby marked public patch and ordinary Foraging job details. **[Take the job]** → begin *Yarrow Cut* through normal plant identification, sustainable harvesting and return-to-scale interactions.

**Dialogue branching:** If the player makes the first optional remark, Benny answers. The follow-up question about the danger zones is available only after that answer; otherwise the interaction moves directly to the convergence line. This keeps the ecological concerns discoverable without forcing a long speech before an ordinary job.

## Intent and boundaries

**Why the earlier exchange was insufficient:** “Cut the good stems and leave the roots” expressed only an efficient harvesting instruction. Nothing in Benny's voice showed why he cared or how deeply. The player asked a purely procedural question (“flowers, or whole stems?”), so the exchange could have belonged to almost any plant buyer. The revised optional exchange makes his personal concern audible, connects conservation to continued work and medicine, and lets the player ask naturally about less fortunate plants and animals.

**Benny's character, LOCKED in principle by the user's instruction:**
- He **loves herbs and the wider living world**. This is genuine affection, not an eccentric joke or a business ploy.
- He believes **preserving the little nature left is vital to prosperity**: future harvests, medicine and ordinary livelihoods depend on living patches.
- He feels sorrow for **plants and animals in the dangerous zones**, which he regards as deprived of human care and affection. This is his compassion, not a claim that he has personally visited the zones or knows every danger inside them.
- He can still work as a practical herb dealer, make sales, weigh bundles and give harvest orders. **Careful harvesting is compatible with affection**; wasteful stripping is not.
- He should not lecture every time he appears. Let concern emerge from a crushed sprig, a depleted patch, an injured creature or a worker cutting too deeply. No catchphrase, invented biography, routine monologue or unapproved new ecology mechanism.

The optional conversation is longer only if the player shows interest. Benny does not suddenly know about the refuse, bones or warm ground at the later outfall. His instinct to protect nature can influence how he reacts to that discovery **when that later scene is separately drafted and approved**, without pre-deciding his knowledge or the player's branch.

Keep exact gathering requirements, patch pressure and job timing in the normal Foraging interface. No quantity, medical recipe or harvesting skill gate is fixed here. The *Warm Patch* discovery waits until after the normal first-job interactions.

Stage 2 will define the reusable gathering work; Stage 3 will reveal the already approved *Warm Patch* incident. Both remain separate user approvals.
