From Claudia's **[Begin the dressings round]**, show the standard clinical job screen. The two assigned beds are near the ward entrance; both need basic dressing attention, but **neither is dying or requires difficult surgery**.

Do not introduce named patients, new disposable item definitions, special medical starter gear, a doctor's diagnosis from a glance, or fixed numerical Healing effects. Both patients can receive appropriate basic care with a sensible use of the supplies already present. An overlooked risk should allow correction, not create an unavoidable injury penalty to a helpless NPC.

## S02-B — Inspect before treating (reusable Trauma assessment)

**[Inspect the wounds]** opens ordinary bed-by-bed examination. The player sees the cut's mild bleeding and the scrape's dirty outer wrap. Use the same basic condition/cleanliness concepts intended for normal Trauma work.

For each bed, the player can **[Check the dressing and skin]** or **[Review the ward note]**. Both lead to truthful information; checking a note alone never makes a wound magically clean. When an unexpected alarming finding appears in the eventual generic job system, **[Call the Matron]** remains a safe escalation; a first-job player is not made to improvise a difficult medical intervention.

**Ordinary task prompt:** “Both patients need attention. Which should you treat first?”

- **[Attend to the seeping cut first]** — deal with the mild active bleeding promptly, then clean and redress the scrape.
- **[Check the dirty dressing first]** — inspect/clean the scrape promptly, then attend to the cut. The temporary delay is visible, but the injury here is minor enough not to turn an acceptable ordering choice into a lethal mistake.

This is an ordinary triage/time decision, not a false dilemma between two deaths, and there is no mandatory moral score. Both patients receive care.

## S02-C — Use clean supplies with a real trade-off

Clean water and linen are **scarce but sufficient** for treating both assigned minor wounds when used reasonably.

**[Plan the dressings]** presents two legitimate ways to arrange the same supervised task:

| Choice | Physical difference | Visible cost/consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Cut only what each wound needs]** | Prepare smaller clean pieces for the two sites | Extra handling/preparation time, less unused clean linen |
| **[Use ready-sized dressings]** | Apply the ward's already prepared standard dressings | Faster, but uses more of the limited stock |

Both use clean material and do not ration away medically necessary care. If the selected method strains remaining supply, the job displays the shortage **before** application and permits **[Adjust the dressing size]** or **[Ask for more linen]**, with time/ledger consequences. No secretly unsafe treatment is presented as equivalent.

**[Clean and dress Bed A] / [Clean and dress Bed B]** — apply the ordinary shared Trauma care actions under the client's skill familiarity (Medicine +3 at creation). The interface reflects actual supplies and wound condition; no new minigame or exact Health number is fixed here.

## S02-D — Ordinary complication: the bandage touches a dirty rail

During the dressing work, a loose end of **unused** clean linen slips against the visibly dirty side of the cot. The player sees **which portion touched the rail**. This is a routine mishap, not the surgical transfer plot.

**UI text (proposed, not spoken):** “The loose end of the dressing brushes the dirty bed rail.”

**[Check the linen]** then choose:

- **[Discard the touched piece; take fresh linen]** — quickest safe correction if enough supply remains; increases clean linen used.
- **[Trim off the touched end]** — requires the unaffected portion to be long enough to cover the wound fully, confirmed visibly before cutting; slightly more work time, preserves clean stock.

If the unaffected portion is inadequate, trimming **must not** become a false safe option; show that clearly and allow **[Get fresh linen]**. Never permit directly placing the contaminated section against the wound. The same reusable handling/quality rules may later apply to other dressing jobs.

The patient may wince while the care is done. Claudia need not deliver a sympathetic conversation about it.

## S02-E — Sign off the normal round

After both injuries have been treated and checked:

1. **[Inspect the finished dressings]** — confirm that both are secure, clean and appropriately sized. If one is loose, ordinary adjustment remains possible.
2. **[Record treatment and linen used]** — work sheet shows which beds received care and the actual supplies consumed, with any discarded/trimmed section documented.
3. **[Report the round to Claudia]** — brief exchange; she cares about correct records and avoiding responsibility for missing materials.

*Claudia scans the dressing count without looking up.*

**CLAUDIA:** “Both done?”

**PLAYER:** “Yes. I had to throw out some linen. It touched the bed rail.”

**CLAUDIA:** “Then write it down. I won't answer for missing dressings.”

**[Close the ward round]** — mark normal work complete exactly once. This round is an **unpaid charity-bed cover** as displayed at entry, yielding **0 coins, 0 ordinary Medicine XP and no loot**. The player does not acquire a reusable bandage kit or permanent medical inventory; materials were the infirmary's. The original +3 Medicine came from character creation.

**Dialogue state guardrail:** The brief exchange above is **only** for a run that actually discarded linen. If the player trimmed the affected end instead, use the alternative acknowledgement:

**PLAYER:** “Yes. The linen slipped, but I cut off the dirty end.”

**CLAUDIA:** “Put the waste on the sheet. I won't have the count come up short.”

Both paths then converge at **[Close the ward round]**. If the complication is resolved without consuming extra material, the ledger still notes the trimmed waste accurately. No dialogue falsely assumes the player chose a different correction.

## Work system and QA boundaries

- Generic repeatable sequence: inspect → choose order → choose dressing preparation → apply appropriate care → fix ordinary soiled-linen mishap → verify → chart/report.
- Put mandatory interactions within the first two minutes of the job session per [Jobs and Time](../../jobs/jobs-and-time.md); world time continues while decisions are open. Do not spring a required click at the end of a long background timer.
- One work-entry panel may summarize multiple simple actions; avoid forcing repeated one-click treatment of an entire ward or creating a complex medical simulation only for this tutorial.
- Honest quality/record outcomes should be retained, but both legitimate supply methods finish safely with the existing ward supplies or a valid ward request.
- Wounds in this scripted shift are **minor, nonsexual and nongraphic**. The true violence/ethical crisis is the separate Stage 3 patient exploitation, not a punishment of first-job clinical mistakes.
- Never invent a combat injury for the player, an infection mechanic, a permanent ward funding system, an automatic patient death, an unapproved patient name, or a new Stitched-quality bandage product.
- The charity-account no-wage explanation is an **approved local contract/accounting circumstance** for this one unpaid cover; exact future ward billing and any relation to the Teaching Bed's coal/medicine funding remain Stage 3/4 authored content, not silently set here.
- Ordinary repeatable *Morning Dressings* shifts outside onboarding pay normal coins and award Medicine XP. The player's next +1 **different Major Skill** comes only when they choose a Stage 3 branch.
