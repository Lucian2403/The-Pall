# Millhand — Journal Voice Review: Stages 1–4

**Status: proposed (2026-10-23), not approved.** No scene dialogue, combat logs or mechanics change. The Journal is a personal first-person account, using only actions and evidence the player actually experienced.

## Stage 1 — At the workshop

**After taking the work paper:**

*Guther's put me on the council's worker plates. Got to check the numbers and set the stamps first. I can manage that.*

**Only if the player asked about wages:**

*Asked when we get paid. After the inspector signs, Guther says. He doesn't seem too sure of that himself. Well, the plates won't mark themselves.* 

**Only if the player also pressed Guther about trusting the council:**

*I asked whether he believed them. Said no, then told me to believe him. Can't say I got much comfort from that.*

## Stage 2 — Marking the plates

**After setting the stamps:**

*Got the number from the work paper and set the stamps. One plate has a bent corner. I'll have to deal with that before the lot's finished.*

**Only one of these, after the choice is carried out:**

- **Straightened:** *I straightened the corner and used the same plate. Took more work, but I didn't waste the metal.*
- **Replaced:** *Put the bent plate aside and took another. Quicker than fixing the old one.*
- **Stamped as-is:** *I stamped the plate with its corner still bent. I knew it wasn't lying flat.* Only after a real stamp; don't pre-write the result.

**If the resulting number was faint:**

*Part of that number was too faint to read. Had to fix it before handing the work in.*

**Only if actually corrected:**

- **Restamped:** *Set the plate steady and stamped the faint part again. The number's clear now.*
- **Another plate:** *Put the badly marked plate aside and used another. This one's clear.*

**When the work is finished:**

*The plates are marked and checked. I'd like to be paid for this, but the inspector still has to approve the lot.*

## Stage 3 — The Missing Stamp

**After reading the slip:**

*The council recorded this worker as dead after the workshop fire. I copied down the number. Why send it back with our plates?*

**After looking at the returned plate:**

*The returned plate has the same number. Someone from the council inspected it, and there's a date beside the mark.*

**Only after examining both pieces of evidence:**

*That's the number I stamped today. They recorded the worker as dead, but the inspector's mark came later. The dates don't match. Something's wrong here.*

**After speaking to Guther:**

*Showed Guther the papers. He saw the dates, but couldn't explain them. Says he won't sign the lot until the council settles it, so we still won't be paid. Grand.*

**One route choice only:**

- **Restamp / Industry:** *I'll stamp the worker number again and take the plates to the depot. I'm leaving the inspector's mark alone.*
- **Contracts / Commerce:** *I'm going to Contracts. They sent the work paper. Someone there ought to know why we're stamping a dead man's number.*
- **Records / Scholarship:** *Wrote down the number. I'll check the other council records myself. Guther's got nothing else here that could explain it.*

No exact worker numbers or dates are invented; the same values must appear across the inspected records and Journal when the game provides them.

## Stage 4 — Three second-job routes

Only include entries from the player's **chosen route**, and only after the related action or discovery actually happens.

### Depot / Industry

**After choosing how to transport the plates:**
- **Secured together:** *Fastened the plates together to carry them to the depot.*
- **Smaller loads:** *Carried the plates in smaller groups. More trips, but less weight at once.*

**After the mismatch:**

*Delivered metal plates to the depot. Someone had entered “workers” on the delivery list. Workers! The clerk could see what I'd brought.*

**After the completed receipt:**

*The depot recorded the goods as plates in the end. I've got a receipt, but I still don't know why that other entry was wrong. No pay for this job, either.*

### Contracts / Commerce

**After reading both official entries:**

*One entry treats the man as dead for compensation. In the other they still charge for him as if he were alive. Both have council seals. How can they accept both?*

**Only the chosen method:**
- **Seal check:** *I asked the clerk to check both seals. Both entries had been accepted. Still no explanation.*
- **Written complaint:** *I marked what was wrong on the work paper. The clerk recorded my complaint.*

**After submitting:**

*Got the complaint recorded, but the clerk couldn't change either entry or approve the plate lot. Still the same question, and still no wages.*

### Records / Scholarship

**After the date comparison:**

*Found the worker in the death register. Then I found the same number in later wage records. They were still using it after he died.*

**After noticing damage:**

*Someone scraped writing from an entry. The archive assistant didn't know who had done it. I wasn't going to damage the page any further.*

**Only the actual comparison:**
- **Neighbouring entries:** *I compared the entries around the damaged one. The number is still there in the later wage record.*
- **Shift book:** *I checked the shift book against the wage record. Both have that number after the recorded death.*

**After recording the finding:**

*Wrote down what I found. Death, wages afterward, and that scraped line. No idea who changed it. I doubt anyone in that room will explain it to me.*
