## What Dogman wants — and what he knows

The Bellrunner was given an ordinary council round, encountered an illegal-looking shift order for a closed fume-affected workplace, and carried out one of three follow-ups. At the exterior service gate, **Dogman wants the courier to stop passing information around and hand over whatever dispatch papers or personal notes they still have**. He is attempting to silence interference with the night shift, not collecting a fee and not acting as an official city guard.

Dogman's only asserted source of authority is that he **works for the Shift Marshal**. He cannot show a written order empowering him to confiscate council papers. His claimed employer **does not appear**; do not turn the Shift Marshal into an encounter boss or prove that the Shift Marshal personally ordered the unsafe mechanism. Dogman knows the courier has been around the site and made trouble for the shift; **he does not automatically know what the clerk, attendant or mechanic wrote or found**. His questions let the player volunteer their own route-specific fact, if they choose.

**Physical paperwork varies by branch; do not make an impossible demand:**
- **Contracts / Deliver:** the watchman already accepted the **sealed night-shift order** in Stage 3; Dogman cannot demand that the Bellrunner produce the same sealed letter. The courier still has the **route sheet** and an authored record of the attendance inquiry in the quest journal; a disputed certificate remains with the counter unless implementation later explicitly approves carrying a copy.
- **Trauma / Warn:** the sealed order's final authorized handoff was not completed; whether the packet is still carried as a quest prop must be kept consistent with Stage 3 implementation. The player has the route sheet and remembers what happened at the infirmary; the medical register stays with staff.
- **Industrial Machinery / Inspect:** likewise, the sealed order remains a pending quest prop unless returned through a separately authored interaction. The machine sketch and retaining pin stay on site; the player knows what was observed without holding a part or carrying the mechanic's work record.
- The confrontation never depends on the presence of one particular sealed letter. Dogman asks for **the route sheet and anything the courier has written down**. He can be refused even if only the route sheet is physically present. The journal/quest state is not a stealable inventory object.

**Approach:** he waits at the existing exterior service passage near the worksite, a plausible common homeward route after any Stage 4 task. He is not shown spying inside an infirmary, reading the attendance book or supervising the mechanism. His precise clothes, physique and weapon design remain unapproved, although he carries no visible city-guard identification in this proposal.

## S05-A — Show Your Papers: first post-task interaction

**Trigger:** selected Stage 4 job finishes. The courier leaves the shift office, first-aid point or external mechanical panel and reaches the passage by the site's service gate. Once only; no encounter before completion of the selected second task.

*Dogman steps away from the service gate and stops the courier.*

**DOGMAN:** “You're the runner who brought the night notice?”

**PLAYER:** “Yes. What do you want?”

**DOGMAN:** “Let's see your papers. The route sheet, and anything you've written down.”

**PLAYER:** “Who are you working for?”

**DOGMAN:** “The Shift Marshal.”

**PLAYER:** “Are you one of his guards?”

**DOGMAN:** “No. I work for him. Now let me see what you've got.”

The practical point: **Dogman freely claims to work for the Shift Marshal, but disclaims being an actual guard**. He is neither a municipal officer nor a person entitled to seize the courier's papers. He has not identified a new authority under which this demand is lawful.

### Player interaction: assess the demand or use your branch's fact

Offer three visible ways to answer. Each preserves the courier's physical papers and exposes Dogman's lack of official authority without a hidden skill check. They differ in what the player says, not in a secret morality meter.

**Choice 1 — [Ask to see his orders]**

**PLAYER:** “Have you got anything in writing that says I have to give them to you?”

**DOGMAN:** “No. I've told you who I work for.”

**PLAYER:** “That's not the same thing.”

The absence of a written confiscation order is an inspectable factual result, not a legal ruling on every guard in the city. Dogman is no less threatening for lacking one.

**Choice 2 — [Keep hold of the route sheet]**

**PLAYER:** “You can look at the route sheet while I hold it. I'm not handing it over.”

**DOGMAN:** “I don't need you to read it to me. I want to know what you've been doing here.”

This is a **non-surrendering display**, not a forced loss of quest documents. The player is a working courier responsible for the route sheet; they're not instructed to forge or destroy it.

**Choice 3 — [Tell him what happened] (route-specific)**

The choice label is common, but present **only the matching player/NPC exchange** below. Dogman learns the branch's details **from this conversation**, not from an unshown secret report.

**If Attendance Certificate / Commerce:**

**PLAYER:** “I took the letter to the watchman. Then I went to the shift office. They're marking men absent for refusing to go into a closed building.”

**DOGMAN:** “You were sent to deliver a letter. You didn't need to go asking about the shift book.”

His response admits neither authorship of the wage policy nor prior knowledge of which entries were marked.

**If First Aid Line / Medicine:**

**PLAYER:** “The gate says the place is closed, and men were standing there coughing. I told them not to go in. Then I helped at the infirmary.”

**DOGMAN:** “You shouldn't have told them to stay out. They can make up their own minds.”

Dogman's priority is that the shift proceed. He does not know what the attendant entered for their bills unless the player says so.

**If The Bell Linkage / Mechanics:**

**PLAYER:** “The safety shutoff was down, but the bell was still working. I went with the mechanic to see why.”

**DOGMAN:** “Then you should have left it to the mechanic. You had a delivery to make.”

He knows the courier was near the works, but **does not claim to know the pinned-link detail** or who moved it.

**Common convergence after any response:**

**DOGMAN:** “You're finished here. Give me the papers and go.”

**PLAYER:** “I'm leaving. I'm not giving you my papers.”

At this point the player has learned who Dogman says he works for and that the demand has no displayed official authorization. The interaction concludes **without** submitting or losing documents. Dogman steps into the lane ahead of the player, leading immediately to the second interaction.

## S05-B — Gate Barred: second post-task interaction

**Location:** the narrow exterior service passage leading back to the public street, immediately beside the condemned block. The player is **not trapped inside the hazardous building**.

*Dogman steps into the way. The courier turns toward the open street, but Dogman moves to block it. A work cart stands nearby.*

**PLAYER:** “I'm done talking. Let me go home.”

**DOGMAN:** “Put the papers down.”

**PLAYER:** “No. They're not yours.”

**DOGMAN:** “Then I'll take them off you.”

*Dogman reaches toward the courier's bag. The courier pulls back. He draws a short club.*

**PLAYER:** “Don't touch my bag.”

**DOGMAN:** “Then stop pulling away.”

**Scene logic:** the assault is **Dogman's choice**, not triggered because the player clicked an aggressive dialogue option or somehow owes him a debt. No peaceful handover is required to progress; all three earlier responses lead here because Dogman wants to silence a courier he cannot lawfully command. The club is a **proposed ordinary hand weapon**, with exact combat item and damage deferred until the shared combat system is designed. No invented firearm, special gear or unique Dogman power.

## Why the previous choice still matters

All routes converge on a single antagonist because each disturbed the operation of the condemned site in a different way: questions at the attendance counter, warnings and aid to workers, or inspection of the bell's safety linkage. Dogman cares that somebody from the delivery round **has not simply gone away**. His exact accusation and the evidence the player can explain differ, but **he is not granted information from paths the player didn't take**.

Dogman does not reveal who authorized the unsafe night shift, who pinned the bell linkage, who chose the attendance or infirmary classifications, or whether the Shift Marshal himself knew each detail. Stage 5 has a local antagonist and practical stakes; it is not an all-purpose conspiracy confession.

## Local outcome scope and handoff to Stage 6

The **two dialogue/interaction encounters are Stage 5**. The turn-based fight is **Stage 6**, not a third mandatory work session. The player's immediate objective is to get past Dogman and keep their papers and personal property; it is **not** to punish the Shift Marshal or solve every contradiction in the city.

**On victory (direction only, not locked fight scripting):** Dogman has to back off, and the courier gets out of the service lane with the local quest findings intact. Dogman's threat fails **in this encounter** without a permanent city-guard faction reaction or invented wider crime system.

**On defeat (direction only):** the player is hurt and is brought toward ordinary medical care through a plausible, safe interruption; exact aid timing and defeat reaction are Stage 6 decisions. Do not permanently confiscate the route sheet, erase quest findings, take the starting coins or softlock access to treatment. This must remain a playable fight with the universal starter kit and no forced extra weapon.

The fight leads to the previously locked shared clinic → home → rent progression. Stage 5 does not write that medical scene or invent rent deadlines.

## Dialogue quality check

- **Player speaks plainly:** “Yes. What do you want?”, “Are you one of his guards?”, “I'm done talking. Let me go home.” The courier doesn't recite the legal doctrine behind an unofficial guard's demand.
- **Dogman speaks as hired muscle:** he wants the route sheet and to stop the courier, and he says so. He does not quote council regulations or offer a polished speech about corruption.
- **Route callbacks use witnessed facts:** a clerk's attendance entry, sick workers and a first-aid visit, or the mechanic's inspection. No actor becomes all-knowing between scenes.
- **Conflict grows physically:** demand → refusal → blocked exit → grab for bag → exposed club → player chooses a stance. The violence follows from Dogman's escalation, not a manufactured Player “attack” button.
- **No comic catchphrase, actor caricature, invented personal history, modern office idioms, unexplained rhetorical shorthand or gratuitous extra exchanges.**
