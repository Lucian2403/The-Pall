## Scope and dramatic purpose

The Bellrunner has completed the ordinary courier interactions, inspected and delivered routine notices, and now has one final sealed order to present. Its destination is an industrial block visibly **closed for unsafe fumes**. Yet the **night-shift bell rings** and a watchman is waiting to admit workers.

The incident should be proved by things in the world, not an ungrounded character recollection or supernatural tease: **a public closure notice, an active shift summons, and the bell**. The worker's decision is immediate. Do not require a return journey to Hector while the night crew waits at the gate.

Stage 3 concludes once the player commits to one of the three already-locked second-job routes. Stage 4 performs the selected job. Dogman's two later interactions, fight, clinic and home are untouched.

## B03-A — The last address (APPROVED)

**Trigger:** Stage 2 reaches its ordinary courier work summary, then the player presses **[Take the final notice]**. At the marked work block:

*An official closure notice is fixed to the gate. A watchman waits beside it. Several workers gather on the street. From inside the block, the shift bell rings.*

**Two inspections, either order, with no skill check:**

1. **[Read the closure notice]** — the block is **officially closed to work after an unsafe-fume incident**. The municipal seal and closure reason are plainly legible. Exact date/issuing office/text TBD.
2. **[Check the dispatch slip]** — the council's **still-sealed order** bears an external routing/dispatch slip showing this same block, an instruction for the **night shift to report**, and a requirement for an **authorized receiving mark**. The courier can see the contradiction without opening confidential correspondence. Exact copy and dates TBD.

The ringing bell is already audible; it does not require a separate puzzle action. After both inspections, optional UI finding: **“Closed for fumes. Night shift called in anyway.”** This is **proposed UI text**, not NPC speech.

These are three distinct objects. The closure notice is not the sealed order; its external dispatch slip is not the interior text; the active worksite bell is not the ordinary duty bell at Hector's Dispatch House. Nothing here yet establishes who reconnected the bell or why. That belongs to the later **Mechanics / Industrial Machinery** branch.

## B03-B — The watchman (APPROVED exact dialogue)

*The watchman looks at the packet in the courier's hand.*

**WATCHMAN:** “Night notice?”

**PLAYER:** “This place is closed.”

**WATCHMAN:** “It is.”

**PLAYER:** “Then why's the bell ringing?”

**WATCHMAN:** “They told me to let the night crew through.”

**PLAYER:** “Who's signing for the notice?”

**WATCHMAN:** “The office is shut. I can take the paper. Can't give you their mark.”

*The waiting workers look toward the gate. One coughs.*

**Why he speaks:** The watchman expects the order and has instructions to admit workers, but he has no power to reopen a closed office or certify its receiving seal. He is not Dogman, a lawful city guard, a named major NPC or the Shift Marshal. He does not know the hidden mechanical cause or confess to illegal arrangements.

The player can optionally select **[Compare the orders]** to read the closure and shift summons together again. It is a clarity action, not a hidden test, and cannot gate the choice. Stage 2's mundane smeared receiving mark cannot hide this contradiction.

## B03-C — An exceptional round without wages (APPROVED causal explanation)

**Approved tutorial-specific contract practice:** Council courier rounds are approved for payment **as a complete signed route**. This particular route contains a final delivery to a **legally closed worksite** whose receiving office is shut. The watchman may accept the packet, but **cannot issue the required office receiving mark**. An informal acknowledgment is not valid certification.

Consequently, the council refuses to clear **the whole exceptional round**, not merely this stop, even if every ordinary Stage 2 receipt was properly obtained. The player is not being punished for choosing the crowded street or leaving a spoiled mark unresolved. The unfairness is that one city authority sends workers to a closed site while the approving office refuses to recognize the work of delivering that order.

**Approved first-job resolution fields (exact UI wording TBD; after route commitment):**
- Shift Dispatch: round not accepted.
- Reason: receiving office closed; authorized final mark unavailable.
- Payment: **0 coins**. Ordinary XP: **0**. Inventory/loot: **none**.

No wages have been promised or secretly removed. No fictitious debt or repayable wage is created. Outside onboarding, **ordinary Dispatch House jobs still pay coins and XP**. Neither handing over the letter nor helping the crew should magically reopen the receiving office, and there is no requirement to forge a signature to solve the tutorial.

If the player later visits Hector, his optional exchange below gives the same reasoning in a human conversation. The ledger already explains the outcome without forcing a trip home or back to the Dispatch House before an urgent second task.

## B03-D — Three choices at the gate

After the inspections and watchman exchange, offer **all three**, with no skill or gear prerequisites. The meanings and jobs are **already locked**; the exact player lines and immediate actions below were **approved on 2026-10-15**. This is a consequential branch choice, unlike Stage 2's two route options.

### A. Deliver the notice as ordered → Commerce / Contracts

**[Deliver the notice]**

**PLAYER:** “You were told to take it. Here.”

**WATCHMAN:** “I'll see it gets inside.”

The watchman accepts the sealed order but cannot give the closed site's official receipt. Following the courier order is **not an endorsement of the building's safety**.

**Stage 4 handoff:** an accessible **shift-office records counter**, *separate from the shuttered worksite receiving office*, still handles attendance records. The courier goes there to reconcile the contested delivery and its sign-in paperwork.

- **LOCKED job:** *Attendance Certificate* — **Commerce → Contracts**.
- **LOCKED later revelation:** a worker refusing the unsafe shift is recorded as **voluntarily absent**, losing wages.
- **Why the second tutorial task is unpaid (PROPOSED):** the player is pursuing an unresolved, rejected council delivery/attendance claim, **not taking a newly authorized paid Contracts commission**.

The separate shift office can enter worker attendance without being able to issue the missing worksite receipt. Do not collapse these into the same office or retroactively validate the original round.

### B. Warn the waiting crew → Medicine / Trauma

**[Warn the crew]**

**PLAYER:** “The block's closed for fumes. Don't go in.”

*A worker coughs. Others turn to read the closure notice.*

At least one worker already has breathing trouble; Stage 3 does not assert that the protagonist can diagnose its cause by sight. Past exposure or a local leak may explain symptoms, but no exposure mechanic or specific diagnosis is canonized here. The player does **not** need to enter the unsafe interior without protection.

**Stage 4 handoff:** an unnamed **working infirmary attendant** recruits the player to help sort coughing workers by urgency at a nearby first-aid point.

- **LOCKED job:** *First Aid Line* — **Medicine → Trauma**.
- **LOCKED later revelation:** the medical record classes the coughs as **non-work illness**, passing treatment costs to workers.
- **Why the second tutorial task is unpaid (PROPOSED):** the courier volunteers immediate help during a hazardous shift dispute; there is **no paid medical appointment or employment contract**.

Do not conflate the workers' medical charges with the player character's later separate **10-coin clinic visit**.

### C. Investigate why the bell rings → Mechanics / Industrial Machinery

**[Inspect the bell controls]**

**PLAYER:** “If they've shut the block, that bell shouldn't be calling anyone in.”

*Service linkage and ventilation-control access are visible from outside the sealed work area.*

The player notices an inspectable external control route, not the reason for its condition. An unnamed **city-works mechanic** supervises checking the linkage and safety shutoff using normal machinery interactions. No special tool or permanent component enters the player's inventory; access stays outside the dangerous interior.

- **LOCKED job:** *The Bell Linkage* — **Mechanics → Industrial Machinery**.
- **LOCKED later revelation:** the bell was **deliberately kept operational after the safety shutoff**, enabling the crew to be called without the building being legally reopened.
- **Why the second tutorial task is unpaid (PROPOSED):** this is an examination of a disputed safety installation **without a paying repair work order**, not an ordinary commissioned service job.

Do not reveal the deliberate bypass in Stage 3, automatically stop the bell or name whoever arranged it.

## Common branch-commit rules (LOCKED economy/progression)

- The player receives **+1 level exactly once**, at **confirmed commitment**, in the branch's different Major Skill: **Commerce / Medicine / Mechanics** respectively. This is not a regular XP reward or a subskill tier. **Industry +3** was already received at character creation.
- First tutorial work task: **0 coins, 0 ordinary XP, no loot**. Chosen second tutorial task: **0 coins, 0 ordinary XP**. Later Dogman fight: **no coins, XP or loot**.
- All three routes keep the character's identical starter inventory and 20 coins until the shared later 10-coin clinic charge. No new permanent gear, criminal status, profession lock, reputation, guaranteed conspiracy continuation or rent consequence is assigned by this choice.
- Record branch identity and local evidence only. Different branch choices give Dogman different later grounds for intimidation but **do not reveal the other branches' specific discoveries**.
- The player may inspect the evidence before confirming. A retry or reconnect must never award +1 again, substitute a branch or roll back the first-job closure.
- The selected Stage 4 job and its supporting NPC may start locally; do not force a trip back to Hector before treating sick workers, meeting the attendance clerk or checking bell controls.

## B03-E — Hector's later return exchange (APPROVED, nonblocking)

**Placement:** when the character **next naturally visits the Dispatch House after dealing with the immediate condemned-site tasks**. It is drafted now because Hector is the established recurring job-giver, but it is **not** a required interlude before Stage 4 or the gate confrontation.

*Hector looks over the returned route sheet.*

**PLAYER:** “They won't clear my last round?”

**HECTOR:** “No. That last office is shut. There's no mark for it.”

**PLAYER:** “You sent me there.”

**HECTOR:** “I sent you where the council put on the sheet. I've put the round down as disputed.”

**PLAYER:** “So there's no pay.”

**HECTOR:** “Not for that round. There's other work on the board.”

Hector acknowledges his dispatch role without inventing advance knowledge of the closure, promising he can override council approval or implying he deliberately put the courier at risk. His answer has one restrained point of friction: the courier followed his route and is nonetheless unpaid. The other work on the board is ordinary compensated Industry/Hauling work, not a new tutorial grant. Do not force the player to seek this conversation to finish onboarding.

## Editorial check

“Night notice?” is what a watchman who wants the paper would ask. “It is.” refuses to pretend the closure doesn't exist. “They told me to let the night crew through” conveys limited instructions, not stupidity or villainy. “I can take the paper. Can't give you their mark” gives the pay problem a concrete cause. “You sent me there” and Hector's answer create small, earned friction without copying Guther's manner or turning work talk into a speech.

There is no forced joke, false-worker-death callback, invented named NPC, fabricated political hierarchy or expository soliloquy.
