# Millhand — Guther Anvil, Approved Opening Conversation

**Status: APPROVED AND LOCKED (2026-10-14).** The exact Scene M01 dialogue was approved by the user as the tonal reference for The Pall. Follows the user-locked [The Pall Spoken Dialogue Standard](../interactions/dialogue-style-guide.md). The previous scene is rejected as too scripted, instructional and stuffed with aphorisms. The **approved story, Guther's name, first task, money rules and later Missing Stamp reveal are unchanged**.

## Specific problems in rejected first version

- **"You've done this kind of work before."** The foreman need not tell a worker their own history. Spoken exposition for a player, not a person.
- **"Numbers straight, stamps clean, no clever shortcuts."** A neat training checklist impersonating dialogue.
- **"You don't mend ink with iron."** A self-conscious proverb; real work instructions should be more direct.
- **"City men can hold a purse tighter than a rivet."** Mechanically themed metaphor/forced comic effect, not a straight response about withheld money.
- **"I need plates, not a handful of you."** A joke in a sequence of jokes. No quiet space for character or friction.
- **"A hammer keeps a miserable, stubborn rhythm"** and **"trying to make its crooked figures behave"** are over-written atmospheric narrator lines. Replace with a plain observable action.
- Three convenient questions and three tidy explanations are a job UI masquerading as a conversation.

## Why the second draft still needs a slight adjustment

- **"No. But that's what it says."** This answers the trust question neatly and closes the disagreement. The response is too tidy: no small social cost to having asked. Guther should evade responsibility by pushing the question back toward the person who controls the money. A tiny edge is enough; do not add a second joke or a longer argument.
- **"Council wants another lot of worker plates. Docket's on the bench."** This already works as plain work talk; do **not** manufacture a quip here.
- **"Check the number before you strike. If one doesn't match, leave it for me."** This is a functional closing instruction and should remain intact.

## Scene M01 — LOCKED dialogue

*Guther lays the council docket beside the blank plates.*

**GUTHER:** "Council wants another lot of worker plates. Docket's on the bench."

**PLAYER (optional):** "And the pay?"

**GUTHER:** "When their inspector accepts the lot."

**PLAYER:** "You trust that?"

**GUTHER:** "No. You can ask him."

**GUTHER (convergence, also used if player skips the pay exchange):** "Check the number before you strike. If one doesn't match, leave it for me."

**Interface action:** **[Take the docket]** → standard Smithing interaction: inspect plate and recorded number; prepare/align die; perform work and resolve a routine complication.

The player can **skip the optional payment exchange** and simply take the docket. The first-task interaction and later locked narrative are identical in either case. Do not ask three mechanical questions just to fill a dialogue menu.

## Performance and design notes

- **One very small, dry point of friction:** Guther openly does not trust the inspector, but when pressed he offers the worker the unpleasant option of asking the inspector directly. There is no explicit joke; the uncomfortable humour is in how casually Guther hands the problem back.
- This small exchange gives Guther a dry, matter-of-fact stance, **not a confirmed catchphrase**, additional backstory, or universal voice template for every character.
- The foreman does not identify or foreshadow the fresh stamp or dead worker by name. **The Missing Stamp reveal happens only after** normal Smithing work, according to the approved structure.
- In UI, the tutorial job's payment state may be labelled **“Pending council acceptance”**, but this is **proposed UI copy**. The scripted tutorial ends with **0 coins, 0 ordinary XP** because the plate's inspection dispute freezes payment. Avoid suggesting a payout will come later in this episode.
- Neither the exchange nor the optional response grants skill or currency; Craftsmanship +3 was assigned at character creation. The second-task branch later gives one +1 level to that other Major Skill.
- Keep action descriptions physically observable. No florid summary of what the worker feels; no modern slang, faux-19th-century parody or scripted puns.
- **Do not pad this with further banter.** The user wants slightly more uncomfortable humour and friction than the second draft, almost imperceptibly. Judge the scene by how it sounds aloud, not by how many punchlines it contains. The exact Scene M01 dialogue is **LOCKED** and should not be revised without user approval.
