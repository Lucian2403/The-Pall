# Salvaging System

Status: PROVISIONAL foundation.

## Core principle

Salvaging is a major economic activity available throughout the world.

Salvageable opportunities should exist in:
- the city;
- protected outskirts;
- Grey Marches;
- Pall zones.

The activity should appear through:
- jobs;
- ruins;
- abandoned machinery;
- damaged vehicles;
- workshops;
- demolition;
- accidents;
- world events;
- expedition discoveries;
- combat aftermath where appropriate.

Salvaging is not a single "Salvage -> wait -> receive Scrap" action.

The player inspects a target, decides what is worth recovering, chooses how deeply to dismantle it, and accepts tradeoffs between time, risk, tool wear, carrying weight, and preservation of valuable parts.

## Skill ownership

Primary progression:
- Fieldcraft -> Scavenging

Strong supporting skills:
- Mechanics
- Industry
- Craftsmanship
- Scholarship for unusual/Pall machinery

Scavenging answers:
- What is worth taking?
- Where are hidden recoverable parts?
- How much can I recover efficiently?

Mechanics answers:
- How does this mechanism come apart?
- Which component is still usable?
- How do I remove it without damaging it?

The best salvage outcomes may therefore require both.

## Salvage difficulty tiers

Salvage targets have a difficulty/complexity rating rather than MMO rarity.

Examples:

### Simple
- broken furniture;
- scrap piles;
- demolition waste;
- basic tools;
- damaged carts.

Recoverable by beginners.

### Mechanical
- pumps;
- clocks;
- workshop machinery;
- firearm mechanisms;
- valves;
- pressure equipment.

Requires better Scavenging and/or Mechanics.

### Complex
- precision industrial machinery;
- advanced respirator assemblies;
- vehicle mechanisms;
- research equipment;
- pressure-control systems.

Requires substantial progression, tools, and often more time.

### Pall / anomalous
- contaminated machinery;
- Pall-modified devices;
- sealed pre-Pall apparatus;
- unusual expedition finds.

May require specialist knowledge, protection, decontamination, or research.

## What higher Salvaging should improve

Progression should visibly improve:

- identification of valuable parts;
- number of usable parts recovered;
- material waste;
- recovery speed;
- ability to access hidden/internal components;
- ability to dismantle complex mechanisms;
- chance of preserving fragile components;
- starting durability/condition of recovered serialized mechanisms;
- contamination recognition;
- estimate of weight/value before committing;
- ability to choose between intact recovery and destructive stripping.

Higher skill should not create extra matter from nowhere.

Better yield represents:
- less breakage;
- less material discarded;
- better separation;
- discovering parts a novice overlooks.

## Three classes of salvage output

To avoid inventory over-complexity, salvage output is divided into three broad classes.

### 1. Bulk salvage

Examples:
- Scrap Metal
- Copper-bearing Scrap
- Timber salvage
- Cloth
- Glass fragments

These are stackable resources.

They do not need individual durability.

### 2. Standard usable components

Examples:
- Pressure Valve
- Precision Spring
- Gear Assembly
- Brass Fitting
- Filter Housing

Once successfully recovered as "usable", these can generally remain stackable/fungible for Phase One.

A failed recovery may instead yield bulk scrap.

### 3. Complex mechanisms

Examples:
- intact pump assembly;
- precision regulator;
- advanced firing mechanism;
- research instrument;
- unusual Pall apparatus.

These may be serialized item instances with:
- current durability;
- current maximum durability;
- contamination;
- repair/refurbishment needs;
- provenance.

This is where recovered condition meaningfully matters.

Do not give every screw and spring its own durability record.

## Example: City salvage job

A workshop fire leaves damaged machinery behind.

The player is hired to clear it.

Visible targets:
- collapsed shelving;
- scorched pressure pump;
- broken tool cabinet;
- cracked gauge assembly.

A novice may identify:
- Scrap Metal
- Timber
- broken tools

A skilled Scavenger may also notice:
- reusable brass valve;
- copper coil;
- intact spring assembly.

A skilled Mechanic may be able to remove the pressure regulator intact instead of smashing the pump for scrap.

The employer may pay wages and allow the worker to keep specified salvage, or the job may pay bonuses based on recovered value.

## Example: Grey Marches ruin

The player finds an abandoned pumping station.

Possible targets:
- seized main pump;
- control cabinet;
- collapsed pipe rack;
- locked maintenance compartment.

The player cannot practically recover everything.

Choices:
- strip copper quickly;
- remove valves carefully;
- spend time opening the cabinet;
- attempt to extract the heavy pump intact;
- mark the site and return with a cart/carriage.

This turns carrying capacity and logistics into part of salvaging.

## Example: Pall salvage

A corroded pressure apparatus sits in a contaminated ruin.

Novice:
- recognizes only bulk metal and obvious danger.

Experienced Scavenger:
- identifies a potentially valuable sealed regulator.

Mechanics specialist:
- understands how to isolate pressure before removal.

Pall Research specialist:
- recognizes contamination pattern and whether the regulator is safe to bring back.

Possible reward:
**Precision Pall Regulator**
- Durability: 43 / 70
- Contaminated
- Requires city decontamination and specialist repair

The same object could instead be destroyed for:
- Cleanable Alloy Scrap
- Brass Fittings
- Copper Wire

## Intact vs destructive salvage

Many targets should allow a choice:

### Strip for materials
- faster;
- easier;
- produces bulk salvage;
- destroys complex component.

### Careful dismantling
- slower;
- more tool wear;
- skill dependent;
- chance to recover valuable usable component.

### Recover intact
- highest potential value;
- very heavy/bulky;
- may require cart, team, carriage, lifting tools, or return expedition.

This creates meaningful decisions rather than a universal best salvage action.

## Salvage interactions

Use the generic authored/procedural interaction framework.

Possible templates:
- seized fastener;
- hidden pressure;
- brittle component;
- trapped contamination;
- unexpected intact mechanism;
- unstable structure;
- jammed access panel;
- tool mismatch;
- false-value component;
- trapped animal/creature;
- previous scavenger cache;
- structural collapse risk.

Routine city salvage should become smoother with skill.

Deep-zone salvage should remain more interactive because the environment itself creates uncertainty.

## Depletion and shared-world behavior

Salvage sites may be:
- one-time authored locations;
- shared persistent ruins with finite recoverable targets;
- procedural event sites;
- renewable city jobs;
- world-event debris;
- temporary expedition sites.

A shared ruin should remember major extraction.

If one player removes the intact generator, another should not immediately find the same generator.

However, routine city jobs and procedural incidents can continually create new salvage opportunities so the profession never runs out of content.

## Economy role

Salvaging provides a bridge between exploration and industry.

It supplies:
- cheap bulk materials;
- components;
- rare mechanisms;
- repair parts;
- unusual research objects.

It should compete with mining/refining in some markets without completely replacing them.

For example:
- fresh Copper Ingots may be more predictable for mass production;
- salvaged copper may be cheaper but irregular;
- intact rare mechanisms may only come from salvage/discovery.

## Design guardrail

Higher Salvaging should make the player think:

"I can see value here that a beginner would miss."

not:

"My loot multiplier is 2.4x."
