# The Pall Combat — Accepted Foundation (APPROVED PRINCIPLES)

**Status: APPROVED PRINCIPLES (2026-10-19); DETAILS AND NUMERICAL BALANCE STILL OPEN.** The user accepted the critique/revised approach in [Part 1's discussion record](core-turns-actions-initiative-momentum-proposal.md) and stated the primary design goal: **individual fights should be demanding and as varied as their circumstances justify, without a complicated core**. This approval does **not** lock every suggested number, attack name, submechanic or untested balance value.

## Essential design identity

- **Turn-based combat**, with **one selected action per actor per round**. Automatic status ticks and the effects of an earlier action are not an additional selectable action.
- **Standard actions** consume zero Momentum; these cover baseline attacking, defending, meaningful movement and essential utility such as reloading, eligible use of ordinary first aid/consumables, and attempting to withdraw. They can still cost Stamina, ammo or supplies.
- **Momentum actions** spend earned tactical advantage to perform stronger, narrower maneuvers or special reactions. Spending Momentum never automatically means a successful hit or status; it also never manufactures missing tools, medical supplies or ammo.
- **Contextual actions** use real objects/terrain/hazards present in an encounter, consume the player's one action, and have situational requirements and proportional costs. Simply moving around a fence or into cover must be accessible to an unarmed beginner without Momentum.
- **Telegraphed, committed enemy intent** lets players plan their one response. Intent visibility varies with the action and situation; the enemy cannot secretly revise an already committed action to exploit the player's choice.
- **Initiative determines actual action order** together with the chosen technique's action speed/priority. The player does **not** always attack before enemies. Defensive protection from Guard/Brace can establish before attack resolution, while using the actor's one action.
- **Guard** can mitigate relevant attacks, but it is neither automatically perfect nor universally immune to bleeding, burns, poison, pressure/steam effects or other statuses. The precise percentage, Stamina cost and scope are **not yet fixed**.
- **Momentum** is a combat resource (not permanent combat XP and not a substitute for Stamina). Attack, effective defense and successful tactical repositioning are candidates for meaningful earning; no idle Guard/Wait farming. Point scale, start value, gains, caps, spend and carryover are pending concrete design.
- **One character Health pool** as already defined; targeting **Head, Torso or Limbs** is an attack choice with hit-risk and potential location-specific injury consequences, not a requirement for three parallel HP bars. Health ceiling reductions and wound effects follow the future shared injury rules.
- **On-hit status rules must qualify**: target and hit/guard/armor conditions must make the status plausible. A high-tier attack is not a universal stun lock. A pressure-system leak belongs to suitable equipment or machinery, not a person's flesh.
- **Nonlethal threat resolution and retreat are real** gameplay results, not scripted victory at a preset round. The frightened young boar can withdraw when safe passage is open; human adversaries can stop fighting without mandatory killing.
- **No automatic tutorial hit or fake wound.** A clean injury-free win leads to the same approved 10-coin clinic examination, with no unnecessary treatment.
- **No arbitrary one-off origin fight mechanics.** All six onboarding enemies use the same shared combat rules; variations arise from enemy aims, weapon characteristics, positioning, surroundings, and encounter victory conditions.

## Keeping the core small while preserving challenge

The design should force choices through a small set of **meaningfully different trade-offs**:
1. **What is the enemy trying to do?** Heavy blow, quick attack, reposition, threat/pressure, defend, disengage, reload, etc.
2. **What can this location really support?** Fence, service cart, narrow passage, coal box, open street, cover or dangerous edge; not necessarily a special interactive object every round.
3. **What counts as ending this encounter?** Drive off a wage enforcer, get out of a boar's path, stop a watchman, survive long enough to withdraw, or other scenario-specific objectives.
4. **What resources are at risk?** Current Health/injuries, Stamina/Fatigue, Composure/Stress/Focus, Momentum, ammunition, equipment condition and possible escape wear — when the mechanics are defined.

Difficulty must follow **readable danger and costly trade-offs**, not invisible accuracy overrides, arbitrary hitpoint inflation, impossible guessing of concealed intentions, or a guaranteed scripted injury. A player who reads a telegraph and chooses well should generally improve the outcome, not invariably win.

## Explicitly NOT yet locked

- The point scale or caps of Momentum (0–100 is a recommended test range); +10/+5 income and 15/20–50 expenses are **examples**.
- Fixed 50% Basic Guard mitigation; precise shield/block/dodge rules; relative effect of Arms/Defense, DEX and Focus.
- Whether Movement and Evasion have separate buttons or action speeds, numerical Initiative formula, per-action timing and tie breaking.
- Telegraph information grades, feint disclosure, multi-foe readability, animation details and range/cover abstractions.
- Damage/armor/critical/status formulas, on-hit probabilities, wounds, bleeding and medical restoration.
- Combat AI patterns, enemy statistics, action balancing and specific weapon load/reload semantics.
- Exact rules for Momentum carryover between fights, nonlethal thresholds, disengagement, escape cost, pressure-system status effects.
