# The Pall — Documentary Industrial Art Direction (Approved 2026-10-22)

**Status: USER-APPROVED VISUAL DOCTRINE.** This is a project-wide direction for illustration, world scenes, NPC portraits, objects and the browser interface. It does **not** certify the v0.2 screenshots or current placeholder assets as final art. Visual details and canon must stay consistent with established lore and individual approved scenes.

## Identity
**Industrial naturalism first, restrained archival/engraved influence second.** Nearly documentary in observation, with only slight departure from realism when it improves coherence, character or legibility. Stylishness comes from editorial discipline and recognisable craft—not fantasy exaggeration, ornamental density, or theatrical lighting.

The city should look lived in and functioning before the player's arrival. Workshop tools must look fit for work, and people must resemble workers rather than posed protagonists. Human-scale subjects (hands, benches, rooms, streets, records and routine labour) carry the setting. Enormous infrastructure can appear, but must not turn every view into a heroic skyline.

## Positive art rules
- Believable proportions, honest manufacturing processes, plausible building joins, used equipment, weathering and wear with a functional cause.
- Observational composition: practical camera placement and eye level where useful; objects presented clearly enough to inspect and use.
- **Documentary light:** soft overcast daylight where present, ordinary window or skylight fill, forge/lamplight only when physically justified. Preserve legibility, midtone detail and natural shadows.
- Material palette driven by soot, dull iron, concrete/stone, damp timber, worn fabric, paper and restrained oxidised metal; avoid uniformly brown/black images. Colours can be subtly selective without cinematic grading.
- Characters with ordinary, distinct faces; practical garments and wear appropriate to role. No glamorisation, exaggerated bulk or decorative fantasy gear.
- Use *B* sparingly in ledger structures, hatch patterns, technical marginalia, municipal forms, map symbols, archival numbering, etched dividers and in-world documents. Most scene and character art remains naturalistic, not an engraving.
- UI has a practical editorial hierarchy: world and interaction first; clean readable type, restrained lines/borders, naturally tactile details, limited metallic accents. Do not mistake faux-industrial ornaments for uniqueness.
- Responsive art includes purposeful desktop/mobile compositions/crops, readable object-scale hotspots and clear focus states.

## Explicit exclusions
No cinematic light, spotlighted faces, heroic rim light, orange–teal grading, bloom, excessive atmosphere/smoke, dramatic shafts from invented light sources, promotional splash-art poses, glossy fantasy UI, brass-overload, clockwork cogs as generic decoration, excessive steampunk machinery, grunge overlays that lower readability, or decorative effects that obscure usable objects.

**Natural practical firelight is not prohibited.** A forge can produce light, but it must behave like a forge, not a theatrical key light.

## Application to first playable Millhand work
- Grand Cylinder City: infrastructural density, working streets and credible industrial civic architecture without invented geography.
- Guther Anvil: grounded foreman, appropriate age and wear, working clothing, practical posture, neutral observational light. His exact appearance is not yet approved.
- Smithing workshop: spatially legible bench, employer-owned plate blanks, numbering dies, hammer, docket and reliable pathways for actual interaction. Do not insert arbitrary serials or dates.
- Documents: municipal legibility, restrained engraved/printed details, believable paper; revealed contents must derive from approved quest state.
- Character / Inventory / Journal: full dedicated destinations with factual game data rather than invented meters or graphic-card dashboards.
- Combat: the same restrained world vocabulary, but threat telegraphs and action outcomes remain clear; no visual treatment changes underlying combat v0.5.1 rules.

## Style verification before asset lock
Assess a matching set of **five** canon-safe assets: city locality, smithing workshop, Guther, workbench plate/dies and a functional browser game screen. Judge documentary plausibility, readability, consistency, everyday material realism, ordinary illumination, mobile cropping and replaceability. A working *style choice* is approved here; **specific final images/fonts/colour codes, portraits and city geometry are still open**.

## Asset pipeline and handover
Each replaceable asset needs: purpose/ID, orientation and target slot, file name, descriptive alt label (when relevant), known depiction constraints, revision and explicit provenance/licensing. Keep placeholders separate from accepted assets. The user will provide character/city references, logos, sound, or archival images **when development reaches the corresponding need**; do not block implementation or request all of them prematurely.

For the first v0.3 scene implementation, generic documentary-style workshop/Guther/object placeholders can be prepared without asking the user to pre-approve exact appearance. A **specific Guther portrait reference or definitive workshop/city composition** will be requested before turning it into canonical production art. **Sound** becomes relevant after the ~1.2-second meaningful-action cadence and event hook interface are tested; then request effect files together with naming/format expectations. Logo/archival references become relevant during later global branding and journal/document polish.

## Preservation / decision scope
This agreement concerns look and presentation. Do not infer or change lore, factions, currencies, attributes, exact item catalogue, job timers, main quests, city districts, NPC identity, source code architecture, server-authoritative outcomes or playable Stage 3–8 completion. The supplied October 22 desktop/mobile city-interface examples specify **layout intent, not approved imagery or mechanics**.

Approved by project owner in conversation on **2026-10-22**. Supersedes exploratory proposal to choose among industrial naturalism, full engraving and cinematic industrial fantasy; cinematic option is rejected.
