# The Pall — Approved Art and Interface Direction

**Status: OWNER-APPROVED CURRENT RENDERING STYLE, 2026-10-23.** This document is the active visual reference. The [earlier documentary-industrial doctrine](../archive/history/00-vision/documentary-industrial-art-direction.md) is preserved as a historical record; its practical industrial-world constraints remain useful, but its former near-photorealistic rendering requirement was superseded.

## Rendering style

The approved **Guther Anvil reference illustration** establishes a stylized, hand-painted MMORPG direction, with the legibility and controlled exaggeration associated with games such as *Albion Online* and *World of Warcraft*. Use these only as **stylistic benchmarks**, never copy their characters, locations, icons, assets or fantasy tropes.

- Strong, clean silhouettes; broad painterly shapes and readable objects at gameplay scale
- Believable human proportions, with restrained exaggeration for clarity
- Tactile, worn materials and selective practical lighting
- Relatively simple surface treatment: **avoid photorealistic microdetail, pinprick lights, visual noise, excessive tiny marks, and decorative clutter**
- Consistent characters, props and environments; each element should read clearly as a reusable illustrated asset

## Setting identity (still binding)

Maintain The Pall's **19th-century-inspired industrial world**: hard labour, soot, used tools, ordinary workers' clothes, worn housing, municipal records, furnaces, pumps, and credible machinery. Distinctive steampunk mechanisms must appear to perform useful work, rather than existing as arbitrary brass decoration.

Grand Cylinder City is huge and densely inhabited. Its visible infrastructure is functional, grimy and economically uneven. Keep the largest architecture legible and avoid crowding every scene with landmarks, glowing windows or miniature flourishes.

## Practical scene and interface rules

- The illustrated scene remains the primary visual surface. **NPC dialogue is a small CRPG-style floating popup within the scene**, not a separate full-width transcript or conversation page.
- The player explicitly chooses when to advance dialogue. Do not auto-advance an exchange in ways that hide or skip approved lines.
- Ordinary equipment and props should be physically plausible and easy to identify and interact with. In the Smithing workshop, keep the metal plates, work paper, numbering stamps and hammer visually distinct.
- On mobile, object-inspection information should appear inline below the visible workbench rather than replace the whole scene.
- Use readable UI hierarchy and functional, restrained decorations. Avoid fake technical dashboards, excessive industrial borders, glossy effects and oversized visual flourishes.
- Equipment layers/portraits and separate transparent assets must remain reusable and consistent with one another.

The ongoing [Workshop Gate 01 UX contract](../05-gameplay/onboarding/millhand/visual-production-gate-01-workshop-ux-contract.md) and [Gate 02 wireframe review](../05-gameplay/onboarding/millhand/visual-production-gate-02-workshop-wireframe-review.md) record narrower screen-specific requirements and owner feedback. A chosen general style does **not** approve any individual final image, geometry, font or UI mock-up.

## Asset handover

For each replaceable asset, record its intended role, orientation/crop, filename, description for accessibility, limitations, revision and provenance/licensing. Preserve accepted source references and do not introduce new lore or game mechanics merely to fill a composition.

Art decisions change **presentation**, not the approved skills, currencies, character history, stage outcomes, equipment physics or server-authoritative rules.
